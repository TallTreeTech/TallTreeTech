Accounting Policies

Last updated: November 14, 2025

This document sets out the accounting policies of Software Systems Limited, trading as TallTree Technologies, in accordance with UK Generally Accepted Accounting Practice (UK GAAP) and applicable company law.

1. Accounting Framework

Basis of Preparation

  • Accounting Standards: FRS 102 (Financial Reporting Standard applicable in the UK and Republic of Ireland)
  • Legal Framework: Companies Act 2006
  • Measurement Basis: Historical cost accounting
  • Going Concern: Financial statements prepared on a going concern basis

Financial Year

Financial Year End: 31 March

Annual accounts prepared for the 12-month period ending 31 March each year.

Currency

  • Functional and presentation currency: Pounds Sterling (GBP)
  • Amounts rounded to the nearest pound unless otherwise stated

2. Revenue Recognition

Revenue is recognised when control of goods or services is transferred to the customer, in accordance with FRS 102 Section 23.

Consulting Services - Time and Materials

  • Recognition: As services are performed
  • Measurement: Based on hours worked at contracted rates
  • Invoicing: Monthly in arrears or as specified in contract
  • Work in Progress (WIP): Unbilled hours accrued at period end

Consulting Services - Fixed Fee Projects

  • Recognition: Percentage of completion method
  • Measurement: Based on milestones achieved or deliverables completed
  • Progress Assessment: Reviewed monthly by project managers
  • Contract Assets: Revenue recognised in excess of amounts invoiced
  • Contract Liabilities: Advance payments deferred until earned

Retainer Arrangements

  • Recognition: Over the period of the retainer agreement
  • Measurement: Straight-line basis over contract term
  • Unused Hours: Non-refundable fees recognised in contract period; refundable fees deferred

Software Licenses (if applicable)

  • Perpetual licenses: Recognised at point of delivery
  • Subscription licenses: Recognised over subscription period
  • Support and maintenance: Recognised over service period

Multiple Element Arrangements

  • Transaction price allocated to performance obligations based on standalone selling prices
  • Each element recognised according to its specific revenue recognition criteria

3. Expense Recognition and Categorisation

Expenses are recognised in the period in which they are incurred on an accruals basis.

Cost of Sales

  • Direct labour costs (consultant salaries and contractor fees for billable work)
  • Third-party software or services purchased for specific projects
  • Direct travel expenses for client projects
  • Subcontractor costs

Operating Expenses

Category Includes
Personnel Costs Salaries, NIC, pension, benefits, training (non-billable time)
Office & Premises Rent, utilities, insurance, maintenance, cleaning
IT & Technology Software subscriptions, cloud services, hardware maintenance, internet
Professional Fees Accountancy, legal, consulting, professional indemnity insurance
Marketing & Business Development Website, advertising, events, promotional materials
Travel & Entertainment Non-billable travel, client entertainment, business development
Administrative Stationery, postage, telephone, banking fees
Depreciation Depreciation of fixed assets (see Section 4)

Prepayments and Accruals

  • Expenses paid in advance are recorded as prepayments and recognised over the period benefited
  • Expenses incurred but not yet invoiced are accrued at period end
  • Materiality threshold for accruals: £500

4. Capitalisation Policy

Assets are capitalised when they meet the recognition criteria and exceed minimum thresholds.

Capitalisation Thresholds

Asset Category Threshold Useful Life
Computer Equipment £1,000 2 years
Software Licenses £1,000 3-5 years
Office Furniture £500 5 years
Office Equipment £250 4 years
Leasehold Improvements £2,000 Over lease term

Items Below Threshold

  • Expensed immediately in the period purchased
  • Recorded in asset register for insurance and tracking purposes (if value exceeds £200)

Software Development Costs

  • Research phase: Expensed as incurred
  • Development phase: Capitalised if technical and commercial feasibility demonstrated
  • Internal use software: Capitalised during application development stage
  • Amortisation: Over estimated useful life (typically 3-5 years)

5. Depreciation Methods and Rates

Depreciation is calculated to write off the cost of fixed assets over their estimated useful lives.

Depreciation Method

  • Primary method: Straight-line depreciation
  • Depreciation commences in the month of acquisition
  • Assets disposed of: depreciation calculated up to the month of disposal

Annual Depreciation Rates

Asset Category Rate Useful Life
Computer Hardware 33.33% 3 years
Computer Software 20-33.33% 3-5 years
Office Furniture 20% 5 years
Office Equipment 25% 4 years
Leasehold Improvements Variable Shorter of improvement life or lease term

Residual Values

  • Assumed to be nil unless significant residual value expected
  • Reviewed annually for material assets

Impairment

  • Assets reviewed for impairment indicators at each reporting date
  • Impairment loss recognised if carrying amount exceeds recoverable amount

6. Invoicing Procedures

Invoice Preparation

  • Frequency: Per contract terms (typically monthly for T&M, upon milestones for fixed-fee)
  • Authorisation: Project manager approval required before issuance
  • Timing: Issued within 5 business days of month-end or milestone completion
  • Numbering: Sequential invoice numbers (format: INV-YYYY-NNNN)

Invoice Requirements

  • Company name, address, and registration number
  • VAT registration number
  • Invoice number and date
  • Customer details and purchase order reference (if applicable)
  • Description of services provided
  • Breakdown of charges (hours, rates, or milestone description)
  • VAT analysis
  • Payment terms and due date
  • Bank details for payment

Time and Materials Invoices

  • Include detailed timesheet summary by consultant
  • Breakdown by task or work package if required
  • Expenses itemised separately with receipts attached if over £50

Fixed Fee Invoices

  • Reference to contract and milestone achieved
  • Evidence of milestone completion if required by contract
  • Percentage of total contract value (for tracking)

7. Payment Terms

Standard Payment Terms: 14 days from invoice date

Alternative Terms

  • 7 days: For small invoices (under £1,000) or short engagements
  • 30 days: For established clients with approved credit terms
  • Advance payment: May be required for new clients or large projects (typically 25-50%)

Late Payment

  • Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 applies
  • Interest chargeable at 8% above Bank of England base rate
  • Debt recovery costs may be charged
  • Work may be suspended for invoices overdue by 30+ days

Payment Methods

  • Bank transfer (preferred)
  • Cheque (payable to Software Systems Limited)
  • Corporate credit card (for approved clients)

8. Bad Debt Provisions

Provision Methodology

Specific provision approach based on age and collectability assessment:

Age Provision % Action Required
0-30 days overdue 0% Payment reminder
31-60 days overdue 10% First chase call/email
61-90 days overdue 25% Formal demand letter
91-120 days overdue 50% Senior management contact, suspend work
121+ days overdue 100% Debt collection or legal action

Specific Circumstances

  • Customer in liquidation/administration: 100% provision unless administrator confirms payment
  • Disputed invoices: Assessed case-by-case; provided if dispute appears valid
  • Known financial difficulties: Higher provision based on assessment

Write-Off

  • Debts written off after all reasonable collection efforts exhausted
  • Typically after 180 days unless legal action in progress
  • Requires director approval for amounts over £2,500

9. VAT Treatment

VAT Registration

Company is VAT registered. Standard VAT rate applies to most services (currently 20%).

Output VAT (Sales)

  • Standard rate (20%): Consulting services, software development, most professional services
  • Zero rate (0%): Not applicable to our services
  • Exempt: Not applicable to our services
  • Outside scope: Services to non-UK customers (subject to reverse charge)

Input VAT (Purchases)

  • VAT reclaimed on business expenses and purchases
  • Blocked input VAT: Entertainment (non-staff), non-business expenses
  • Partial exemption: Not applicable (no exempt supplies)

International Services

  • B2B services to EU: Reverse charge applies (customer accounts for VAT)
  • B2B services to non-EU: Outside scope of UK VAT
  • B2C services: Generally subject to UK VAT unless customer outside UK and belonging basis applies
  • Evidence required: VAT number validation, customer business address

VAT Returns

  • Submitted quarterly via Making Tax Digital (MTD)
  • Due one calendar month and 7 days after period end
  • Payment via direct debit or bank transfer

10. Foreign Currency Transactions

Initial Recognition

  • Transactions recorded at spot rate on transaction date
  • For practical purposes, average monthly rate may be used for numerous small transactions

Period-End Translation

  • Monetary items (receivables, payables): Retranslated at period-end rate
  • Non-monetary items: Not retranslated (kept at transaction date rate)
  • Exchange differences recognised in profit and loss

Foreign Currency Bank Accounts

  • Translated at period-end closing rate
  • Exchange gains/losses recognised in profit and loss

Hedging

  • Forward contracts may be used for significant foreign currency commitments
  • Hedge accounting applied where hedge relationship documented and effective

11. Record Retention

Minimum retention period: 6 years from the end of the accounting period

Financial Records

Record Type Retention Period
Annual accounts and audit files Permanently
VAT records 6 years
Invoices (sales and purchase) 6 years
Bank statements 6 years
Payroll records 6 years after tax year
Expense claims and receipts 6 years
Contracts and agreements 6 years after expiry
Asset registers 6 years after disposal

Storage

  • Electronic records stored securely with regular backups
  • Physical records stored in locked, fireproof cabinets
  • Access restricted to authorised personnel
  • Secure destruction at end of retention period

12. Approval Authorities

Financial transactions require appropriate authorisation based on value and type.

Expenditure Approval Limits

Amount Authoriser
Up to £500 Project Manager or Department Head
£501 - £2,500 Director
£2,501 - £10,000 Two Directors
Over £10,000 Board approval required

Capital Expenditure

  • Under £5,000: Director approval
  • £5,000+: Board approval with business case

Contracts and Commitments

  • Service contracts under £10,000 annual value: Director approval
  • Service contracts £10,000+: Board approval
  • Client contracts: Director approval (all values)

Bank Account Operations

  • Payments up to £10,000: Single director signature
  • Payments over £10,000: Two director signatures
  • Payroll payments: Single director signature (after payroll verification)

13. Reconciliation Procedures

Bank Reconciliations

  • Frequency: Monthly, within 5 business days of statement
  • Prepared by: Bookkeeper or finance staff
  • Reviewed by: Director or Finance Manager
  • Outstanding items: Investigated and cleared promptly

Receivables Reconciliation

  • Aged debtor reports prepared monthly
  • Reconciled to sales ledger control account
  • Reviewed by Finance Manager
  • Follow-up actions assigned for overdue amounts

Payables Reconciliation

  • Aged creditor reports prepared monthly
  • Reconciled to purchase ledger control account
  • Supplier statement reconciliations for significant suppliers (quarterly minimum)

VAT Reconciliation

  • VAT control account reconciled before each return submission
  • Input VAT and output VAT analysed and verified
  • Supporting documentation checked for sample transactions

Payroll Reconciliation

  • Monthly reconciliation of gross pay to P32 (employer payment record)
  • Annual reconciliation to P60s
  • Pension contributions reconciled to pension provider statements

14. Internal Controls

Segregation of Duties

  • Separate functions: authorisation, recording, custody, reconciliation
  • No single person controls all aspects of a transaction
  • Where segregation not possible (small team), compensating controls applied

Access Controls

  • Accounting system access restricted by user role
  • Bank account access limited to directors
  • Regular review of user permissions

Document Controls

  • Sequential numbering of invoices and credit notes
  • Blank cheque stock secured
  • Supporting documentation required for all transactions

15. Reporting Requirements

Management Accounts

  • Frequency: Monthly
  • Issued: Within 10 business days of month end
  • Contents: P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, KPIs, variance analysis

Annual Accounts

  • Prepared in accordance with FRS 102
  • Filed at Companies House within 9 months of year end
  • Audit requirement assessed annually based on size criteria

Tax Returns

  • Corporation Tax (CT600): Filed within 12 months of accounting period end
  • VAT returns: Quarterly via MTD
  • PAYE returns: Monthly or quarterly as applicable

16. Policy Review and Compliance

  • Policy reviewed annually by directors and accountants
  • Updated for changes in accounting standards, tax law, or business operations
  • All finance staff trained on policies and procedures
  • Compliance monitored through internal reviews and external audit (if applicable)
