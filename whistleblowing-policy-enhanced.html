Whistleblowing Policy

Software Systems Limited trading as TallTree Technologies

Last updated: November 14, 2025

Purpose: This policy encourages and supports employees and workers to raise serious concerns about wrongdoing or danger in the workplace without fear of reprisal. The Company is committed to the highest standards of openness, probity, and accountability.

1. Policy Statement

The Company is committed to conducting business with honesty and integrity. We expect all employees, officers, contractors, and stakeholders to maintain these high standards.

However, all organizations face the risk of things going wrong or of unknowingly harboring malpractice. A culture of openness and accountability is essential to prevent such situations occurring or to address them when they do.

This policy:

  • Encourages employees to report suspected wrongdoing as soon as possible
  • Provides clear channels for raising concerns
  • Protects individuals who raise genuine concerns from victimisation or detriment
  • Ensures concerns are taken seriously and investigated appropriately
  • Complies with the Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998 (PIDA)

2. Legal Framework

2.1 Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998 (PIDA)

The Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998 (incorporated into the Employment Rights Act 1996) protects workers who make "protected disclosures" from suffering detriment or dismissal.

Protected Disclosure Requirements:

A disclosure is "protected" if the worker:

  1. Reasonably believes the disclosure is made in the public interest
  2. Reasonably believes the information disclosed, and any allegation contained in it, are substantially true
  3. Makes the disclosure to an appropriate person/body (see section 5)
  4. Discloses information about one of the qualifying categories of wrongdoing (see section 3)

2.2 Protection from Detriment

Workers who make protected disclosures are protected from:

  • Dismissal (dismissal for making a protected disclosure is automatically unfair)
  • Detrimental treatment (harassment, victimisation, discrimination)
  • Disciplinary action for making the disclosure

These protections apply from day one of employment, with no qualifying period.

3. What Constitutes Whistleblowing

3.1 Qualifying Disclosures

Whistleblowing is the disclosure of information about wrongdoing that the whistleblower reasonably believes is in the public interest. Under PIDA, qualifying disclosures concern:

Categories of Wrongdoing:

1. Criminal Offences

  • A crime has been, is being, or is likely to be committed
  • Examples: Fraud, theft, bribery, corruption, money laundering, tax evasion

2. Failure to Comply with Legal Obligations

  • Breach of legal or regulatory obligations
  • Examples: Breach of contract, employment law violations, data protection breaches, failure to comply with professional regulations

3. Miscarriages of Justice

  • A miscarriage of justice has occurred, is occurring, or is likely to occur

4. Danger to Health and Safety

  • Health or safety of any individual has been, is being, or is likely to be endangered
  • Examples: Unsafe working conditions, inadequate safety procedures, failure to conduct risk assessments

5. Damage to the Environment

  • The environment has been, is being, or is likely to be damaged
  • Examples: Illegal waste disposal, pollution, environmental regulation breaches

6. Cover-Up of Wrongdoing

  • Information about any of the above has been, is being, or is likely to be deliberately concealed

3.2 What is NOT Covered by This Policy

This whistleblowing policy is not for:

  • Personal grievances: Complaints about your own employment (pay, treatment, working conditions) - use the Grievance Procedure
  • Interpersonal disputes: Disagreements with colleagues - use informal resolution or Grievance Procedure
  • Minor policy breaches: Minor non-compliance with company policies that do not constitute serious wrongdoing

If you are unsure whether an issue should be raised under this policy, err on the side of caution and raise it - the Company will determine the appropriate route.

4. Who is Covered by This Policy

This policy applies to:

  • All employees (permanent, fixed-term, part-time)
  • Contractors and consultants

  • Volunteers and interns

5.2 What to Include in Your Report

When raising a concern, provide as much detail as possible:

  • Nature of concern: What wrongdoing you believe is occurring
  • Background and context: How you became aware of the issue
  • Individuals involved: Who is involved or responsible (if known)
  • Dates and timeline: When the wrongdoing occurred or is occurring
  • Evidence: Any documents, emails, or other evidence supporting your concern
  • Witnesses: Names of anyone else who may have information
  • Previous action: Whether you have raised this concern before and what happened

You do not need to prove the wrongdoing - you need only have a reasonable belief that it is occurring.

5.3 Anonymous Reporting

The Company accepts anonymous reports via the whistleblowing email or postal address.

Anonymous Reporting:

  • You may raise concerns anonymously if you wish
  • Anonymous concerns will be investigated, though investigation may be limited if we cannot seek further information from you
  • Consider providing a means for us to contact you anonymously (e.g., anonymous email address, postal box)
  • The Company encourages you to identify yourself to allow for full investigation and protection, but respects your choice

5.4 Written vs. Verbal Reports

  • Written reports preferred: Email or letter provides clear record
  • Verbal reports accepted: You may raise concerns in person or by phone - the recipient will make a written record
  • Follow-up in writing: If you initially report verbally, consider following up in writing to confirm the details

6. Investigation Process

6.1 Acknowledgment

When you raise a concern:

  • You will receive acknowledgment of your report within 5 working days (unless anonymous)
  • You will be told who is handling the matter
  • You may be asked to attend a meeting to provide further information

6.2 Initial Assessment

The recipient of the report will:

  1. Assess whether the concern falls within the scope of whistleblowing policy
  2. Determine the appropriate investigator (may be internal or external)
  3. Consider whether immediate action is needed (e.g., to prevent ongoing harm)
  4. Decide on investigation approach

6.3 Investigation

Investigation Process:

  • Independent investigator: Investigation conducted by someone independent of the matter (may be external investigator for serious cases)
  • Gather evidence: Review documents, emails, records
  • Interview witnesses: Speak to relevant individuals
  • Interview subject: Person alleged to be involved given opportunity to respond (if appropriate at this stage)
  • Maintain confidentiality: Information shared only on need-to-know basis
  • Document findings: Written investigation report prepared

6.4 Timeline

The Company aims to:

  • Complete initial assessment within 10 working days
  • Complete investigation within 28 days (may be extended for complex cases)
  • Keep you informed of progress (if you are identifiable)

6.5 Outcome

Following investigation, the Company will:

  • Determine whether the concern is substantiated
  • Take appropriate action (disciplinary action, policy changes, referral to authorities, etc.)
  • Provide feedback to you on outcome (subject to confidentiality and legal constraints)

Feedback Limitations: While the Company will provide feedback where possible, we may not be able to share full details of action taken due to:

  • Confidentiality obligations to other individuals
  • Data protection requirements
  • Ongoing legal or regulatory proceedings
  • Employment law restrictions on disclosing disciplinary matters

7. Protection from Retaliation

7.1 No Detriment for Protected Disclosures

The Company will not tolerate any detrimental treatment of individuals who make protected disclosures in good faith.

Prohibited Conduct:

It is unacceptable to:

  • Dismiss or threaten dismissal
  • Subject to disciplinary action
  • Harass, victimise, or intimidate
  • Discriminate against
  • Deny opportunities (training, promotion, etc.)
  • Make threats or take any action detrimental to the whistleblower

Anyone found to have retaliated against a whistleblower will face disciplinary action up to and including dismissal.

7.2 What to Do if You Experience Retaliation

If you believe you are experiencing detriment as a result of making a protected disclosure:

  1. Report immediately to HR or senior management (or whistleblowing hotline if HR involved)
  2. The Company will investigate urgently
  3. Appropriate action will be taken to stop the detriment
  4. Disciplinary action may be taken against those responsible

7.3 Legal Rights

Workers who suffer detriment for making a protected disclosure have legal rights:

  • Bring claim to Employment Tribunal (no qualifying period or cap on compensation)
  • Claim for personal injury if detriment causes physical or psychiatric harm
  • May also have claims against individuals (not just employer) who cause detriment

8. Confidentiality and Anonymity

8.1 Confidentiality

The Company will make every effort to protect your identity:

  • Your identity will not be disclosed without your consent
  • Information will be shared only on a need-to-know basis for investigation
  • Documents will be kept securely

Limitations on Confidentiality:

There may be circumstances where we cannot maintain confidentiality:

  • Legal requirement to disclose (e.g., court order, regulatory investigation)
  • To ensure fair investigation and due process (e.g., allowing subject to respond to allegations)
  • To take disciplinary or legal action

We will inform you if we cannot maintain confidentiality before disclosure (unless prevented by law).

8.2 Anonymity

Anonymous reports are accepted but:

  • Investigation may be more limited without ability to seek clarification
  • Difficult to provide feedback on outcome
  • May be harder to protect you from detriment if we don't know your identity

9. Unfounded or Malicious Allegations

9.1 Good Faith Requirement

Whistleblowers should have a genuine, reasonable belief that wrongdoing has occurred or is likely to occur.

  • You do not need to prove the allegation - only have a reasonable belief
  • Protection applies even if investigation finds concern was unfounded, provided you had reasonable grounds for belief
  • Making a mistake or being wrong does not mean you acted in bad faith

9.2 Malicious or False Allegations

However, deliberately making false or malicious allegations is serious misconduct:

  • Allegations made with knowledge they are false
  • Allegations made with malicious intent to harm another individual
  • Vexatious or repeated unfounded allegations

Such conduct may result in disciplinary action up to and including dismissal and will not be protected under PIDA.

9.3 Distinguishing Good Faith from Malicious

The Company will consider:

  • Did you have reasonable grounds for belief at time of disclosure?
  • Did you genuinely believe the concern was in the public interest?
  • Is there evidence of ulterior motive or malice?

Mere fact that investigation does not substantiate concern does not mean it was malicious.

10. External Reporting and Escalation

10.1 When to Report Externally

The Company encourages internal reporting first, but recognizes there may be circumstances where external reporting is appropriate:

  • You reasonably believe you would suffer detriment if you raised internally
  • You reasonably believe evidence would be concealed or destroyed
  • You have already raised the matter internally and it has not been addressed
  • The wrongdoing is exceptionally serious
  • Senior management is involved in the wrongdoing

10.3 Wider Public Disclosure

In exceptional circumstances, disclosure to the media or other wider public disclosure may be protected if:

  • You reasonably believe the information is substantially true
  • You are not acting for personal gain
  • It is reasonable in all circumstances to make the disclosure
  • AND one of the following applies:
    • You reasonably believed you would suffer detriment if you raised internally or with prescribed person
    • There is no prescribed person and you reasonably believed evidence would be concealed or destroyed
    • You had already raised the matter internally or with a prescribed person

Caution: The threshold for protection for wider public disclosure is high. Consider seeking legal advice before making such disclosure. Contact ACAS or Public Concern at Work (Protect) for guidance.

11. Support for Whistleblowers

11.1 Emotional and Wellbeing Support

Raising a whistleblowing concern can be stressful. The Company offers:

  • Employee Assistance Programme (EAP): Free, confidential counselling and support
  • Occupational health: Support if concern is affecting your health
  • Reasonable adjustments: Workplace adjustments if needed
  • Time off: Reasonable paid time off for meetings, legal advice, etc.

11.2 Procedural Support

  • Right to be accompanied at meetings (by colleague or trade union representative)
  • Regular updates on investigation progress
  • Feedback on outcome (within confidentiality constraints)
  • Opportunity to comment on investigation findings (where appropriate)

11.3 Legal Advice

The Company recommends seeking independent legal advice if:

  • You are unsure whether your concern qualifies as protected disclosure
  • You are considering external disclosure
  • You have experienced detriment
  • You are considering Employment Tribunal claim

Organizations that provide free whistleblowing advice: Protect, ACAS, Citizens Advice.
