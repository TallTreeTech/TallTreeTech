UK Sponsor License Policy

Software Systems Limited trading as TallTree Technologies

Last updated: November 14, 2025

Purpose: This policy sets out the Company's procedures for conducting right to work checks, maintaining sponsor license compliance, and fulfilling reporting duties to the Home Office. Compliance with UK immigration law is a legal requirement and a condition of holding a sponsor license.

Legal Requirement: It is a criminal offence under the Immigration, Asylum and Nationality Act 2006 to employ someone who does not have the right to work in the UK. Penalties include unlimited fines and up to 5 years imprisonment.

1. Overview and Legal Framework

1.1 Applicable Legislation

  • Immigration, Asylum and Nationality Act 2006 - Illegal working offence
  • Immigration Act 2014 and 2016 - Enhanced penalties and compliance
  • Immigration Rules - Sponsor license requirements
  • Home Office Sponsor Guidance - Operational requirements

1.2 Company Commitment

The Company is committed to:

  • Full compliance with UK immigration law and Home Office requirements
  • Conducting thorough right to work checks for all employees
  • Maintaining sponsor license in good standing
  • Supporting international talent through visa sponsorship where appropriate
  • Preventing illegal working

1.3 Scope

This policy applies to:

  • All employees (including directors and contractors)
  • All hiring managers and those involved in recruitment
  • Designated sponsor license holders and compliance officers
  • HR personnel conducting right to work checks

2. Right to Work Checks

2.1 Legal Obligation

The Company must conduct right to work checks before employing anyone in the UK to establish a statutory excuse against illegal working penalties.

When Checks Are Required:

  • Before employment starts: For all new hires (no exceptions)
  • Before existing employees start new role: If substantive change in duties
  • Follow-up checks: When limited leave to remain expires

2.2 Timing of Checks

Critical Timing:

  • Checks must be completed before the first day of employment
  • An offer of employment may be made conditional on right to work check
  • No work may commence until check is successfully completed
  • Failure to conduct checks exposes Company to criminal liability

2.3 Who Conducts Checks

  • Right to work checks conducted by designated HR personnel
  • Checkers must be trained in Home Office right to work requirements
  • Alternative: Use Home Office online right to work checking service (where available)
  • Alternative: Use certified Identity Service Provider (IDSP)

3. Right to Work Check Process

3.1 Types of Right to Work

Individuals may have right to work in the UK through:

List A - Unlimited Right to Work (No Follow-up Check Required)

  • British or Irish citizens
  • Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR)
  • EU Settlement Scheme - Settled Status
  • Certain Commonwealth citizens with right of abode

List B - Time-Limited Right to Work (Follow-up Check Required)

  • Skilled Worker visa
  • EU Settlement Scheme - Pre-Settled Status (expires after 5 years)
  • Graduate visa
  • Student visa (with restrictions)
  • Family visas with work permission
  • Any other time-limited leave to remain with work permission

3.2 Manual Document Check Process

Step 1: Obtain Original Documents

  • Request original documents from the acceptable document list (see Home Office guidance)
  • Documents must be original (not copies)
  • Check document in presence of the individual (in-person or via video call)

Step 2: Check the Document

Document Verification Checks:

  • Validity: Document is in date and not expired
  • Authenticity: Appears genuine, not tampered with or counterfeit
  • Consistency: Details consistent across multiple documents if provided
  • Photograph: Photo is of the person presenting the document
  • Date of birth: Consistent and indicates person is of working age
  • Expiry date: Note expiry date for follow-up check scheduling
  • Work restrictions: Check for any conditions or restrictions on working

Security Features to Check:

  • Holograms, watermarks, and security features present
  • Document feels authentic (quality of paper, printing)
  • No signs of tampering (alterations, photo replacement)
  • Use UV light to check security features where appropriate

Step 3: Copy and Record

What to Copy:

  • Copy all relevant pages of the document
  • For passports: Front cover, photo page, expiry date, visa/endorsement pages
  • For biometric residence permits (BRP): Front and back
  • Ensure copies are clear and legible

What to Record on Copies:

  • Date check was undertaken
  • Name of person who conducted the check
  • Position/title of checker
  • Note if check conducted via video call (and record details)

3.3 Online Right to Work Check (Home Office Service)

For individuals with biometric residence permits, eVisas, or EU Settlement Scheme status:

Online Check Process:

  1. Individual generates share code at: https://www.gov.uk/prove-right-to-work
  2. Individual provides share code and date of birth to employer
  3. Employer accesses Home Office online checking service
  4. Enter share code and date of birth
  5. View right to work profile
  6. Check photo matches the individual
  7. Save or print the response (this is the statutory record)

Advantages of Online Checks:

  • Real-time verification with Home Office systems
  • Reduced risk of document fraud
  • Automatic notification of changes to right to work
  • Acceptable evidence for statutory excuse

See the Home Office's current list of acceptable documents. Common documents include:

List A (Unlimited Right to Work) List B (Time-Limited Right to Work)

Note: The acceptable documents list is subject to change. Always refer to the current Home Office guidance: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-work-checks-employers-guide

3.5 Follow-Up Checks (List B)

For employees with time-limited right to work:

Scheduling Follow-Up Checks:

  • Record expiry date of visa/leave in HR system
  • Set reminder for follow-up check before expiry date
  • Recommended: Check 4-6 weeks before expiry

Conducting Follow-Up Checks:

  1. Contact employee to request updated documents
  2. Conduct new right to work check using same process
  3. Retain new copies and record check date
  4. If visa extended, schedule next follow-up check
  5. If employee awaits decision, check for Certificate of Application

If Right to Work Expires:

  • If no extension granted and leave expires, employment must cease
  • Notify Home Office if sponsored worker's leave expires
  • Do not continue to employ without valid right to work

4. Document Verification and Fraud Prevention

4.1 Detecting Fraudulent Documents

Be vigilant for signs of document fraud:

  • Inconsistencies in fonts, spacing, or formatting
  • Poor quality printing or lamination
  • Evidence of tampering (different photo glued on, alterations)
  • Missing security features
  • Incorrect format for document type
  • Date of birth making person too young to work

4.2 What to Do if Document Suspected Fraudulent

  1. Do not accept the document
  2. Do not accuse the individual of fraud
  3. Request alternative acceptable documents
  4. If fraud strongly suspected, report to Home Office
  5. Do not proceed with employment without valid right to work
  6. Seek advice from compliance officer or HR

4.3 Reporting Suspected Fraud

Report suspected immigration fraud to Home Office:

  • Phone: 0800 788 887
  • Online: https://www.gov.uk/report-immigration-crime

5. Record Keeping Requirements

5.1 What Records to Keep

Mandatory Records for Statutory Excuse:

  • Clear copies of original documents (all relevant pages)
  • Date check was conducted
  • Name and signature of person who conducted check
  • For online checks: Screenshot or PDF of Home Office response
  • For video checks: Details of when and how check was conducted

Additional Records (Best Practice):

  • Right to work check checklist (documenting steps taken)
  • Share codes used (for online checks)
  • Follow-up check schedule and reminders
  • Record of any issues or concerns during check

5.2 How to Store Records

Storage Requirements:

  • Secure storage: Protected from unauthorized access, loss, or destruction
  • Electronic storage: Encrypted, access-controlled HR system
  • Physical storage: Locked filing cabinet (if paper copies)
  • Separate from general personnel files: Right to work records kept separately (recommended)
  • Accessibility: Readily available for Home Office inspection

Data Protection:

  • Right to work checks are lawful under UK GDPR (legal obligation)
  • Copies may contain special category data (nationality, immigration status)
  • Process copies securely and limit access to HR/compliance personnel
  • Do not make unnecessary copies or share widely

5.3 Retention Period

Retention Requirement:

  • Minimum: For duration of employment PLUS 2 years after employment ends
  • This provides statutory excuse against illegal working penalty
  • Records must be retained even if employee leaves quickly
  • After retention period, securely destroy copies

6.2 Duties as a Sponsor

As a licensed sponsor, the Company must:

  1. Compliance: Comply with all sponsor duties and Immigration Rules
  2. Genuine roles: Only sponsor migrants for genuine roles at appropriate skill level
  3. Record keeping: Maintain detailed records on all sponsored workers
  4. Monitoring: Monitor sponsored workers' employment and attendance
  5. Reporting: Report specified events to Home Office within required timeframes
  6. Prevention: Take steps to prevent illegal working
  7. Cooperation: Cooperate with Home Office compliance visits and requests
  8. Key personnel: Appoint and maintain required key personnel

Consequences of Non-Compliance:

  • Suspension or revocation of sponsor license
  • Action plan or compliance visits
  • Downgrade of license rating
  • Civil penalties (up to £20,000 per illegal worker)
  • Criminal prosecution in serious cases

8. Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS) Allocation

8.1 What is a CoS?

A Certificate of Sponsorship is an electronic record (not a physical document) that contains:

  • Details of the job being offered
  • Salary and terms
  • Evidence that the role meets skill and salary requirements
  • Unique CoS reference number for visa application

8.2 When CoS is Required

A CoS must be assigned for:

  • New Skilled Worker visa applications
  • Extensions of existing Skilled Worker visas
  • Changes in employment (new role, significant changes to duties/salary)

8.3 CoS Allocation Process

Before Assigning CoS:

  1. Verify eligibility: Role meets Skilled Worker requirements (RQF Level 3+, appropriate salary)
  2. Right candidate: Individual meets job requirements and has required qualifications
  3. Genuine vacancy: Role is genuine, not created solely for immigration purposes
  4. Approval: Obtain approval from Authorising Officer or designated Level 1 user

Assigning CoS via SMS:

  1. Log into Sponsorship Management System
  2. Select "Assign a certificate of sponsorship"
  3. Complete all required fields accurately
  4. Enter worker's details, job details, salary
  5. Confirm appropriateness of job and salary
  6. Submit and generate CoS reference number
  7. Provide CoS reference to worker for visa application

8.4 CoS Requirements

Requirement Details
Skill level RQF Level 3 or above (A-level equivalent minimum)
Salary threshold Higher of: £38,700 per year OR going rate for occupation code
Occupation code Must select appropriate SOC code matching duties
English language Worker must meet English language requirement (B1 CEFR - usually degree taught in English or English test)

New Entrant Concession: Lower salary threshold (£30,960 or 70% of going rate) available for:

  • Under 26 years old
  • New PhD graduates
  • Postdoctoral researchers
  • Students switching from Student visa

8.5 CoS Records

Maintain records of:

  • All CoS assigned (reference numbers, dates, recipients)
  • Job details and salary offered
  • Approval documentation
  • Outcome of visa applications

9. Sponsored Worker Monitoring

9.1 Monitoring Duties

The Company must actively monitor sponsored workers to ensure:

  • Employment status: Worker remains employed in sponsored role
  • Attendance: Worker is attending work as expected
  • Role compliance: Worker is performing duties as described in CoS
  • Contact details: Current contact details maintained
  • Changes: Any changes to circumstances are identified and reported

9.2 Contact Details

Maintain up-to-date records of sponsored workers' contact details:

  • Home address in the UK
  • Email address
  • Phone number
  • Update SMS within 20 working days of any change

9.3 Absence Monitoring

Monitor and record sponsored workers' absences:

  • Unauthorized absences exceeding 10 working days must be reported
  • Keep records of all absences (authorized and unauthorized)
  • Investigate unexplained absences
  • Report to Home Office if contact is lost with sponsored worker

10. Reporting Duties to Home Office

10.1 Mandatory Reporting Events

The Company must report the following events to the Home Office via SMS:

Event Timeframe
Worker does not start work on expected start date Within 10 working days of expected start date
Worker stops working (resignation, dismissal, etc.) Within 10 working days of last day of work
Unauthorized absences exceeding 10 consecutive working days Within 10 working days of 10th day of absence
Significant change in worker's circumstances (role, salary, hours, work location) Within 10 working days of change
Worker's contact details change Within 20 working days
Suspected breach of immigration conditions by worker As soon as reasonably practicable
Any suspicion of fraudulent application or documents As soon as reasonably practicable
Changes to sponsor circumstances (key personnel, address, etc.) Within 20 working days

10.2 How to Make Reports

  1. Log into Sponsorship Management System (SMS)
  2. Navigate to appropriate reporting section
  3. Select the sponsored worker
  4. Select the event type to report
  5. Provide required details and dates
  6. Submit report
  7. Retain confirmation and record of report made

10.3 Consequences of Failure to Report

Failure to report events can result in:

  • Sponsor license suspension or revocation
  • Downgrade of license rating
  • Action plan imposed
  • Reduced CoS allocation
  • Civil penalties

11.1 Records to Maintain

For each sponsored worker, maintain records of:

Pre-Employment

  • Job advertisement and recruitment process
  • Evidence of qualifications and experience
  • Resident Labour Market Test (if applicable - historical requirement)
  • CoS assignment and details
  • Copy of visa/BRP

During Employment

  • Right to work checks (initial and follow-up)
  • Contact details (current address, phone, email)
  • Attendance and absence records
  • Payslips and proof of salary payments
  • Any changes to employment (role, salary, location)
  • Performance and disciplinary records
  • Training and development records

Post-Employment

  • End date and reason for leaving
  • Report made to Home Office
  • Return of company property

11.2 Retention Period for Sponsor Records

Home Office Requirement:

  • Sponsor records must be kept for duration of sponsorship PLUS 1 year after:
    • Worker stops working for the Company, OR
    • Worker's visa expires, OR
    • Worker obtains settlement (ILR)
  • Records must be readily available for Home Office compliance visits

Note: This is in addition to the 2-year retention for right to work records. Keep sponsor records for 1 year AND right to work records for 2 years after employment ends.

11.3 Accessibility of Records

  • All records stored securely but accessibly
  • Electronic records preferred (searchable, readily available)
  • Must be able to provide records within 5 working days if requested by Home Office

12. Home Office Compliance Visits

12.1 Compliance Visit Overview

The Home Office may conduct compliance visits to:

  • Check sponsor is complying with duties
  • Verify sponsored workers are employed as described
  • Review records and processes
  • Investigate specific concerns

12.2 Types of Visits

  • Announced visits: Notified in advance (usually 1-2 weeks)
  • Unannounced visits: No prior warning given
  • Virtual visits: Conducted remotely via video call and document submission

12.3 What to Expect

During a compliance visit, Home Office may:

  • Interview key personnel and sponsored workers
  • Review sponsor records (CoS, right to work checks, attendance, payroll)
  • Inspect premises and working conditions
  • Check Sponsorship Management System usage
  • Verify contact details and monitoring processes
  • Request to see specific documents or evidence

12.4 Preparation for Compliance Visits

The Company maintains ongoing compliance readiness:

  • Keep all records up to date and organized
  • Conduct internal audits of sponsor compliance quarterly
  • Ensure key personnel are trained and available
  • Maintain SMS account with current information
  • Have documented processes for all sponsor duties

12.5 During the Visit

  • Cooperate fully with Home Office officials
  • Provide requested documents promptly
  • Allow access to premises and personnel
  • Answer questions honestly and accurately
  • Take notes of what is requested and discussed
  • Request clarification if uncertain about requirements

12.6 After the Visit

  • Home Office will provide outcome (usually within 20 working days)
  • Possible outcomes: No action, action plan, suspension, revocation
  • If action plan issued, comply with all requirements within timeframes
  • Address any issues identified immediately

15. Preventing Discrimination

15.1 Legal Protection

Right to work checks must not result in discrimination:

Equality Act 2010: Unlawful to discriminate on grounds of:

  • Race
  • Nationality
  • Ethnic or national origins

Right to work checks must be applied equally to all candidates, regardless of appearance, accent, or perceived nationality.

15.2 Non-Discriminatory Practices

To avoid discrimination:

  • Treat everyone equally: Conduct same check for all candidates
  • Do not assume: Do not assume someone lacks right to work based on name, appearance, or accent
  • Accept all valid documents: Accept any document from Home Office acceptable list
  • Do not request specific documents: Let individual choose which documents to provide
  • Same scrutiny: Apply same level of scrutiny to all documents
  • No additional checks: Do not request additional documents from certain groups

15.3 When to Conduct Checks

To avoid discrimination claims:

  • Conduct right to work checks after offering the job (conditional on right to work check)
  • Do not ask about immigration status during application or interview
  • May ask "Are you eligible to work in the UK?" but must ask all candidates
