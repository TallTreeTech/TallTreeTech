1. Purpose and Scope

1.1 Purpose

This policy establishes the IT governance framework for the Company, defining how IT strategy, decisions, and investments align with business objectives. It ensures that IT resources are used effectively, risks are managed appropriately, and value is delivered to the organization and its stakeholders.

1.2 Scope

This policy applies to:

  • All IT systems, infrastructure, applications, and services
  • All IT investments, projects, and initiatives
  • All individuals involved in IT decision-making and management
  • Third-party IT service providers and vendors
  • Cloud services and external hosting arrangements

1.3 Policy Statement

The Company shall maintain a structured IT governance framework that ensures IT activities support business goals, manage risk effectively, optimize resource utilization, and deliver measurable value. IT governance is the shared responsibility of executive management, IT leadership, and business stakeholders.

2. IT Governance Framework

2.1 Governance Principles

The Company's IT governance is based on the following principles:

Strategic Alignment

IT strategy shall be aligned with and support business strategy. IT investments and initiatives must demonstrate clear business value and support organizational objectives.

Value Delivery

IT shall deliver value to the business through effective and efficient use of resources. The return on IT investments must be measured and optimized.

Risk Management

IT risks shall be identified, assessed, and managed appropriately. Risk management must be embedded in IT processes and decision-making.

Resource Optimization

IT resources (people, infrastructure, applications, information) shall be used optimally to support business needs.

Performance Measurement

IT performance shall be measured against defined objectives and key performance indicators. Continuous improvement is expected.

IT governance operates through a structured decision-making framework:

3. IT Strategy and Planning

3.1 IT Strategy Development

The IT Director shall develop and maintain an IT strategy that:

  • Aligns with and supports the business strategy
  • Addresses current and future business needs
  • Considers technology trends and innovations
  • Defines strategic IT initiatives and roadmap
  • Identifies required resources and investments
  • Addresses IT risks and security requirements

3.2 IT Strategic Planning Process

  1. Business alignment: Understand business strategy, goals, and priorities
  2. Current state assessment: Evaluate existing IT capabilities, systems, and performance
  3. Gap analysis: Identify gaps between current state and required future state
  4. Strategic options: Develop and evaluate potential strategies
  5. Roadmap development: Create multi-year IT roadmap with initiatives and milestones
  6. Resource planning: Identify required people, budget, and infrastructure
  7. Approval: Present strategy to executive management and board for approval
  8. Communication: Communicate strategy to stakeholders
  9. Execution: Implement strategy through planned initiatives
  10. Review: Monitor progress and adjust strategy as needed

3.3 IT Strategic Plan Content

The IT Strategic Plan shall include:

  • Executive summary
  • Business context and drivers
  • Current IT landscape and capabilities
  • Future IT vision and objectives
  • Strategic initiatives and projects
  • 3-year roadmap with priorities and timelines
  • Resource and budget requirements
  • Key performance indicators and success measures
  • Risk assessment and mitigation strategies

3.4 Annual IT Planning

Each year, the IT Director shall develop an annual IT plan that:

  • Translates the IT strategy into concrete initiatives for the year
  • Prioritizes projects and activities
  • Allocates resources and budget
  • Defines deliverables and timelines
  • Identifies risks and dependencies
  • Sets performance targets and KPIs

4. IT Decision-Making and Investments

4.1 Investment Decision Framework

IT investment decisions shall be based on:

  • Business value: Expected benefits and return on investment
  • Strategic alignment: Support for business strategy and objectives
  • Risk: Technical, operational, and financial risks
  • Cost: Total cost of ownership including ongoing costs
  • Feasibility: Technical feasibility and resource availability
  • Urgency: Business criticality and timing requirements

4.2 Investment Approval Thresholds

Investment Value Approval Authority Documentation Required
Up to £5,000 IT Director Informal business justification
£5,001 - £25,000 IT Director + Finance Director Business case summary
£25,001 - £100,000 Executive Management Team Full business case with ROI analysis
Over £100,000 Board of Directors Comprehensive business case, risk assessment, alternatives analysis

4.3 Business Case Requirements

Business cases for significant IT investments must include:

  • Executive summary: Overview of proposal and recommendation
  • Business need: Problem statement and opportunity
  • Proposed solution: Description of IT investment
  • Alternatives considered: Other options evaluated
  • Benefits: Quantified and qualified benefits
  • Costs: Capital and operational costs (3-5 year view)
  • ROI analysis: Return on investment and payback period
  • Risks: Key risks and mitigation strategies
  • Implementation approach: High-level plan and timeline
  • Resource requirements: People, skills, infrastructure needed

5. Risk Management

5.1 IT Risk Management Framework

The Company shall maintain a systematic approach to IT risk management including:

  • Risk identification
  • Risk assessment and evaluation
  • Risk treatment and mitigation
  • Risk monitoring and review

5.2 IT Risk Categories

IT risks shall be categorized as:

Security Risks

Risks related to confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information and systems. Examples: Cyber attacks, data breaches, unauthorized access.

Operational Risks

Risks affecting IT service delivery and operations. Examples: System outages, performance degradation, capacity issues.

Project Risks

Risks affecting IT project delivery. Examples: Budget overruns, timeline delays, scope creep, resource constraints.

Compliance Risks

Risks of non-compliance with laws, regulations, or standards. Examples: GDPR violations, Cyber Essentials non-compliance, licensing violations.

Third-Party Risks

Risks arising from dependence on external suppliers and services. Examples: Vendor failure, service provider security incidents, contract disputes.

Technology Risks

Risks related to technology choices and obsolescence. Examples: Unsupported software, aging infrastructure, emerging threats.

5.3 Risk Assessment

IT risks shall be assessed using a consistent methodology:

Risk Level Impact Likelihood Response Required
Critical Severe business impact Highly likely Immediate action, escalate to board
High Significant business impact Likely to occur Priority mitigation, executive oversight
Medium Moderate business impact May occur Planned mitigation, regular monitoring
Low Minor business impact Unlikely Monitor, accept, or mitigate as practical

5.4 Risk Treatment Options

For identified risks, the following treatment options shall be considered:

  • Mitigate: Implement controls to reduce likelihood or impact
  • Transfer: Shift risk to third party (insurance, outsourcing)
  • Avoid: Eliminate activity that creates the risk
  • Accept: Acknowledge risk and accept consequences (with approval)

5.5 Risk Register

A comprehensive IT risk register shall be maintained including:

  • Risk description and category
  • Risk owner
  • Current risk rating (likelihood x impact)
  • Existing controls
  • Planned mitigation actions
  • Target risk rating (after mitigation)
  • Status and review dates

5.6 Risk Reporting

  • IT risk register reviewed monthly by IT management
  • High and critical risks reported to executive management monthly
  • Comprehensive risk report to board quarterly
  • Significant new risks escalated immediately

6. Change Management

6.1 Change Management Principles

All changes to production IT systems must be:

  • Properly authorized
  • Assessed for risk and impact
  • Tested before implementation
  • Documented and tracked
  • Capable of being rolled back if needed
  • Communicated to affected users

6.2 Change Categories

Change Type Examples Approval Required Testing
Standard Change Pre-approved routine changes (e.g., security patches, password resets) Pre-authorized As per standard procedure
Normal Change Most changes requiring assessment and approval Change Advisory Board or IT Director Required in test environment
Emergency Change Urgent changes to restore service or address critical security issues IT Director + retrospective CAB review Best effort given urgency

6.3 Change Management Process

  1. Request: Change requested via change management system
  2. Assessment: Impact, risk, and resource requirements evaluated
  3. Approval: Approved by appropriate authority based on risk and impact
  4. Planning: Implementation plan created including rollback procedures
  5. Testing: Change tested in non-production environment
  6. Scheduling: Change scheduled considering business impact
  7. Communication: Users notified of upcoming change
  8. Implementation: Change executed according to plan
  9. Verification: Success confirmed, systems monitored
  10. Documentation: Change recorded and closed in system
  11. Review: Post-implementation review for significant changes

6.4 Change Advisory Board (CAB)

The CAB shall:

  • Meet weekly to review proposed changes
  • Assess change risks and impacts
  • Approve or reject changes
  • Resolve scheduling conflicts
  • Review failed changes

CAB Membership: IT Director (chair), System Administrators, Business Representatives

7. Asset Management

7.1 IT Asset Inventory

The Company shall maintain a comprehensive inventory of IT assets including:

  • Hardware (servers, computers, network devices, mobile devices)
  • Software (applications, licenses, operating systems)
  • Cloud services and subscriptions
  • Network infrastructure
  • Data and databases

7.2 Asset Information

For each asset, the following information shall be recorded:

  • Asset identification (unique ID, serial number)
  • Asset type and description
  • Owner and custodian
  • Location
  • Purchase date and cost
  • Warranty and support information
  • Configuration details
  • Security classification
  • Status (in use, spare, retired)

7.3 Asset Lifecycle Management

Acquisition: Assets procured following approval processes, recorded in inventory
Deployment: Assets configured securely and assigned to users or services
Operation: Assets maintained, patched, and monitored throughout useful life
Decommissioning: End-of-life assets retired, data securely erased, disposal documented

7.4 Software Asset Management

  • All software must be properly licensed
  • Software licenses tracked and managed
  • License compliance audited annually
  • Unauthorized software prohibited
  • Software renewals managed proactively

8. Vendor and Supplier Management

8.1 Vendor Selection

Selection of IT vendors and suppliers shall consider:

  • Technical capability and expertise
  • Financial stability
  • References and track record
  • Security and compliance capabilities
  • Service level commitments
  • Pricing and total cost of ownership
  • Contract terms and exit provisions

8.2 Vendor Contracts

IT vendor contracts must include:

  • Scope of services or products
  • Service levels and performance metrics
  • Security and data protection requirements
  • Audit rights
  • Liability and indemnity provisions
  • Termination and transition provisions
  • Pricing and payment terms

8.3 Vendor Performance Management

  • Critical vendors reviewed quarterly
  • Service levels monitored against SLAs
  • Issues and escalations tracked
  • Annual vendor performance assessments
  • Contract renewals evaluated based on performance

8.4 Third-Party Risk Management

Vendors with access to Company systems or data must:

  • Complete security assessment questionnaire
  • Provide evidence of security certifications (ISO 27001, SOC 2, etc.)
  • Agree to security and data protection terms
  • Undergo periodic security reviews
  • Report security incidents to the Company

9. Service Delivery and Operations

9.1 Service Catalogue

The IT department shall maintain a service catalogue documenting:

  • Services provided to the business
  • Service descriptions and features
  • Service availability and support hours
  • How to request each service
  • Service performance targets

9.2 Service Level Management

Service levels shall be defined for key IT services including:

  • Availability targets (e.g., 99.5% uptime)
  • Performance targets (e.g., response times)
  • Incident response times
  • Support hours and escalation procedures

9.3 Service Monitoring

  • IT services monitored continuously for availability and performance
  • Service metrics collected and analyzed
  • Service level achievement measured monthly
  • Reports provided to business stakeholders

10. Disaster Recovery and Business Continuity

10.1 Business Continuity Requirements

IT systems critical to business operations must have:

  • Documented recovery time objectives (RTO)
  • Documented recovery point objectives (RPO)
  • Backup and recovery procedures
  • Disaster recovery plans
  • Regular testing of recovery capabilities

10.2 Data Backup

Data backup requirements:

  • Critical data backed up daily (minimum)
  • Backups stored off-site or in separate cloud region
  • Backup integrity verified regularly
  • Restoration tested quarterly
  • Backup retention per data retention policy

10.3 Disaster Recovery Testing

  • Disaster recovery plans tested annually (minimum)
  • Tests documented with lessons learned
  • Plans updated based on test results
  • Staff trained on recovery procedures

11. Performance Measurement

11.1 Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

IT performance shall be measured using KPIs such as:

Category Example KPIs
Service Delivery System availability, incident resolution time, user satisfaction
Security Patch compliance %, security incidents, time to remediate vulnerabilities
Projects On-time delivery %, budget variance, benefits realization
Finance IT spend as % of revenue, cost per user, ROI on investments
Compliance Audit findings, policy compliance %, certification status

11.2 Performance Reporting

  • Monthly performance dashboard for IT management
  • Quarterly performance report to executive management
  • Annual IT performance review to board
  • KPIs reviewed and updated annually

12. Roles and Responsibilities

12.1 Board of Directors

  • Oversee IT governance framework
  • Approve IT strategy and significant investments
  • Ensure IT risks are appropriately managed
  • Review IT performance quarterly

12.2 Managing Director

  • Ultimate accountability for IT governance
  • Ensure alignment between IT and business strategy
  • Approve IT budget and resource allocation
  • Champion IT initiatives supporting business goals

12.3 IT Director (Policy Owner)

  • Develop and maintain IT strategy
  • Implement IT governance framework
  • Manage IT budget and resources
  • Ensure delivery of IT services
  • Manage IT risks and compliance
  • Report on IT performance
  • Lead IT management team

12.4 IT Management Team

  • Execute IT strategy and initiatives
  • Deliver IT services to agreed standards
  • Manage IT operations and infrastructure
  • Implement security and compliance controls
  • Identify and escalate risks and issues

12.5 Business Department Heads

  • Define IT requirements for their departments
  • Participate in IT governance forums
  • Sponsor IT projects benefiting their areas
  • Support change management and user adoption
  • Provide feedback on IT service quality

13. Compliance and Audit

13.1 Compliance Requirements

The Company's IT systems and processes must comply with:

  • UK Cyber Essentials requirements
  • GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018
  • Software licensing agreements
  • Industry standards and best practices
  • Contractual obligations to clients
  • Internal policies and procedures

13.2 Internal Audits

  • IT controls audited annually (minimum)
  • Audit scope covers governance, security, operations
  • Audit findings tracked with remediation plans
  • Follow-up audits verify remediation
  • Audit results reported to board

13.3 External Audits and Assessments

  • Cyber Essentials certification maintained
  • External IT audits as required by contracts or regulations
  • Third-party security assessments conducted periodically
  • Findings tracked and addressed systematically

14.1 Review Schedule

This policy shall be reviewed:

  • When significant changes occur to business or IT strategy
  • Following major IT incidents or failures
  • When regulatory requirements change
