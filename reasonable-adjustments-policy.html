Reasonable Adjustments Policy

Last updated: November 14, 2025

Policy Statement: TallTree Technologies is committed to providing an inclusive and accessible workplace where all employees can perform to their full potential. This policy outlines our approach to making reasonable adjustments for disabled employees and those with health conditions, in accordance with the Equality Act 2010.

1. Legal Framework

1.1 The Equality Act 2010

Under the Equality Act 2010, employers have a legal duty to make reasonable adjustments for disabled employees or job applicants. This is to ensure they are not placed at a substantial disadvantage compared to non-disabled people.

1.2 Definition of Disability

The Equality Act defines disability as a physical or mental impairment that has a substantial and long-term adverse effect on a person's ability to carry out normal day-to-day activities.

  • Substantial: More than minor or trivial
  • Long-term: Has lasted or is likely to last at least 12 months
  • Normal day-to-day activities: Include mobility, manual dexterity, physical coordination, continence, ability to lift/carry/move everyday objects, speech, hearing, eyesight, memory, ability to concentrate/learn/understand, perception of risk of physical danger

1.3 Protected Characteristics

This policy relates primarily to disability as a protected characteristic. However, reasonable adjustments may also be considered for employees with the following protected characteristics where appropriate:

  • Pregnancy and maternity
  • Religion or belief
  • Age
  • Sex

1.4 Progressive Conditions

People with progressive conditions (such as HIV, cancer, multiple sclerosis, or muscular dystrophy) are protected from the point of diagnosis, even if they don't yet experience substantial adverse effects.

2. Scope and Application

2.1 Who is Covered

This policy applies to:

  • Job applicants
  • Current employees (permanent and contract)
  • Employees returning from long-term absence
  • Employees whose disability or condition develops during employment
  • Employees with fluctuating conditions

2.2 All Stages of Employment

Reasonable adjustments may be required at any stage:

  • Recruitment and selection
  • During employment
  • Promotion or transfer
  • Training and development
  • Redundancy or dismissal procedures

3. When Adjustments Apply

3.1 The Duty to Make Adjustments

The duty arises when the employer knows, or could reasonably be expected to know, that an employee:

  • Has a disability
  • Is likely to be placed at a substantial disadvantage compared to non-disabled people

3.2 Triggering the Duty

The duty can be triggered by:

  • An employee disclosing a disability or health condition
  • Observable indicators (e.g., use of mobility aids, visible symptoms)
  • Absence records or performance issues that may indicate an underlying condition
  • Medical certificates or occupational health reports
  • Information from colleagues or managers

3.3 Anticipatory Duty

While the duty generally arises when we know about a specific individual's needs, TallTree Technologies also takes steps to anticipate the needs of disabled people more generally by:

  • Ensuring our policies and procedures are accessible
  • Providing equipment and technology that can be adapted
  • Creating an inclusive remote working environment
  • Training managers on disability awareness

4. Request and Assessment Process

4.1 How to Request Adjustments

Employees can request reasonable adjustments by:

  • Speaking to their line manager
  • Contacting the HR department at hr@talltree.tech
  • Raising the matter during a one-to-one meeting
  • Submitting a written request

Requests can be made at any time and do not need to be formal. You do not need to use the term "reasonable adjustment" - simply explaining the difficulty you're experiencing is sufficient.

4.2 Initial Discussion

When a request is received:

  1. A confidential discussion will be arranged within 5 working days
  2. This will usually involve your line manager and/or HR
  3. We will discuss:
    • The nature of your disability or condition
    • How it affects you at work
    • What barriers or disadvantages you experience
    • What adjustments might help
    • Any adjustments you've found helpful previously
  4. You may bring a colleague or representative to this meeting

4.3 Information Gathering

To properly assess what adjustments are needed, we may:

  • Ask for your consent to obtain medical information
  • Request a report from your GP, consultant, or other healthcare professional
  • Arrange an occupational health assessment
  • Consult with Access to Work (a government scheme)
  • Seek advice from specialist organizations (e.g., RNIB, RNID, disability charities)

You will be kept informed throughout this process and your consent will be sought before obtaining medical information.

4.4 Assessment Considerations

When assessing requests, we will consider:

  • The nature of the disability and its effects
  • The disadvantage experienced
  • The effectiveness of potential adjustments
  • The practicality of the adjustment
  • The cost (balanced against available resources and funding sources)
  • The impact on the organization and other employees
  • Health and safety implications

4.5 Decision and Implementation

  • A decision will normally be made within 4 weeks of the initial request
  • The decision and reasons will be communicated in writing
  • If adjustments are approved, an implementation plan will be created
  • Where possible, adjustments will be implemented within 4 weeks
  • Some adjustments (e.g., specialist equipment) may take longer due to procurement
  • Temporary measures may be put in place while permanent adjustments are arranged

5. Types of Adjustments Available

Reasonable adjustments are highly individual and will depend on your specific circumstances. Examples include:

5.1 Physical Adjustments and Equipment

  • Ergonomic equipment: Specialist chairs, desks, keyboards, mice
  • Mobility aids: Appropriate workspace setup for wheelchair users
  • Environmental controls: Lighting adjustments, noise reduction
  • Physical aids: Document holders, footrests, wrist rests

5.2 Technology and Software

  • Assistive technology: Screen readers, speech recognition software, screen magnification
  • Alternative input devices: Trackballs, touchpads, eye-gaze technology
  • Communication tools: Video relay services, real-time captioning for meetings
  • Color and contrast adjustments: For visual impairments or dyslexia
  • Text-to-speech and speech-to-text software
  • Specialist software: Mind-mapping tools, grammar checkers, reading aids

5.3 Working Arrangements

  • Flexible hours: Modified start/finish times to accommodate medical appointments or peak functioning times
  • Part-time working: Reduced hours or job-share arrangements
  • Extended or more frequent breaks: For pain management, medication, or fatigue
  • Phased return to work: Gradual increase in hours after absence
  • Modified duties: Redistribution of certain tasks or responsibilities
  • Additional time: For completing tasks or meeting deadlines

5.4 Communication and Information

  • Written summaries: Of meetings or verbal discussions
  • Information in alternative formats: Large print, audio, easy-read
  • Additional time to process information
  • Quieter working environment: For concentration or sensory needs
  • One-to-one briefings: Instead of group settings
  • Visual aids: For understanding complex information

5.5 Support and Supervision

  • Mentoring or buddy system
  • Additional training or coaching
  • More frequent feedback and supervision
  • Modified performance objectives
  • Alternative ways of demonstrating competence
  • Job coach or support worker (may be funded by Access to Work)

5.6 Policies and Procedures

  • Modified sickness absence triggers: To account for disability-related absence
  • Additional leave: For medical appointments or treatment
  • Adapted grievance or disciplinary procedures
  • Alternative assessment methods: In recruitment or performance review

5.7 Remote Working Specific

As a fully remote company, adjustments may include:

  • Allowance for home office adaptations: Contribution to creating an accessible workspace
  • Delivery and setup support: For heavy equipment
  • Virtual communication preferences: Camera-off options, alternative to video calls
  • Flexible meeting schedules: To accommodate medical needs
  • Asynchronous communication: Where real-time interaction is challenging

6. Funding and Costs

6.1 Company Responsibility

TallTree Technologies will fund reasonable adjustments as required by law. The cost of an adjustment is one factor in determining reasonableness, but is not the only factor.

6.2 Access to Work

Access to Work is a UK government scheme that provides grants for practical support if you have a disability or long-term health condition. It can help pay for:

  • Specialist equipment
  • Adaptations to equipment
  • Support workers or job coaches
  • Communication support (BSL interpreters, lip speakers)
  • Travel to work costs

HR will support employees in applying to Access to Work where appropriate. This can significantly reduce costs to the company and enable more extensive adjustments.

6.3 What is "Reasonable"

Whether an adjustment is "reasonable" depends on:

  • Effectiveness: Will it remove or reduce the disadvantage?
  • Practicality: Is it feasible to implement?
  • Cost: What is the financial impact, balanced against company size and resources?
  • Available funding: Can costs be offset by Access to Work or other sources?
  • Impact on others: How does it affect colleagues and the business?
  • Health and safety: Does it create risks?

TallTree Technologies will make all reasonable efforts to accommodate requests. However, we are not required to make adjustments that are not reasonable (e.g., would fundamentally alter the nature of the role, create undue burden, or be impractical).

7. Review and Monitoring

7.1 Regular Reviews

Reasonable adjustments should be reviewed regularly to ensure they remain effective:

  • Initial review after 3 months of implementation
  • Ongoing reviews at least annually
  • Ad-hoc reviews when circumstances change
  • As part of return-to-work or performance review processes

7.2 Changes in Circumstances

You should inform your manager or HR if:

  • Your condition changes (improves or worsens)
  • Your needs change
  • Current adjustments are no longer effective
  • You require additional or different adjustments
  • Your job role or responsibilities change

7.3 Monitoring Effectiveness

We will monitor adjustments by:

  • Regular check-ins with the employee
  • Feedback from line managers
  • Performance indicators
  • Absence records
  • Wellbeing assessments

8. Confidentiality

8.1 Privacy and Data Protection

Information about disabilities and health conditions is sensitive personal data under GDPR and will be handled with strict confidentiality:

  • Information will only be shared on a need-to-know basis
  • Your explicit consent will be sought before sharing medical information
  • Records will be stored securely and separately from general personnel files
  • Access will be restricted to HR and relevant managers only
  • Information will be retained in accordance with data protection law

8.2 Disclosure to Colleagues

  • You control what information is shared about your condition
  • Colleagues may need to know about adjustments (e.g., flexible hours) but not the medical reason
  • We will discuss with you what information should be shared and with whom
  • You may choose to be open about your condition or keep it private

8.3 Medical Records

  • Medical reports will only be obtained with your written consent
  • You have the right to see medical reports before they are sent to the employer
  • You can refuse consent, though this may limit our ability to assess what adjustments are needed

9. Appeals Process

9.1 If Your Request is Declined

If a request for reasonable adjustments is declined, you have the right to appeal:

  1. Submit a written appeal to the HR department within 10 working days
  2. State clearly why you believe the decision is wrong
  3. Provide any additional information or evidence
  4. Suggest alternative adjustments if applicable

9.2 Appeal Process

  • The appeal will be heard by a senior manager not involved in the original decision
  • An appeal meeting will be arranged within 15 working days
  • You may bring a companion (colleague or trade union representative)
  • The appeal decision will be final
  • You will receive a written response within 10 working days of the appeal meeting

9.3 Grievance Procedure

If you remain dissatisfied, you may raise a formal grievance under the company's grievance procedure.

9.4 External Rights

You also have the right to:

  • Seek advice from the Equality Advisory and Support Service (EASS)
  • Contact ACAS for conciliation
  • Bring a claim to an Employment Tribunal (within 3 months minus 1 day of the discrimination)

10. Recruitment and Selection

10.1 Application Stage

  • Job advertisements will include a statement about reasonable adjustments
  • Application forms will ask if candidates require adjustments for the interview
  • We will make reasonable adjustments to the recruitment process
  • Candidates will not be asked health questions before a job offer (with limited exceptions)

10.2 Interview Adjustments

Examples of interview adjustments include:

  • Accessible interview format (phone, video, in-person with adaptations)
  • Additional time for interviews or tests
  • Interview questions provided in advance
  • Alternative assessment methods
  • BSL interpreter or communication support
  • Breaks during lengthy interviews

10.3 Pre-Employment Health Screening

  • Health questions may only be asked after a conditional job offer
  • Questions must be relevant to the job requirements
  • A conditional offer cannot be withdrawn unless there are genuine occupational reasons and reasonable adjustments would not overcome the issue

11. Performance Management and Discipline

11.1 Disability and Performance

  • Reasonable adjustments may include modified performance standards or assessment methods
  • Disability-related poor performance should be addressed through adjustments, not discipline
  • Before taking performance action, consider whether reasonable adjustments have been made
  • Seek occupational health advice if performance issues may be disability-related

11.2 Sickness Absence

  • Disability-related absence should be treated separately from other sickness absence
  • Absence triggers may be adjusted to account for disability-related absence
  • Before taking action on absence, consider whether reasonable adjustments could reduce absences
  • Long-term absence procedures should include reasonable adjustment discussions

11.3 Disciplinary Procedures

  • Consider whether conduct issues are related to a disability
  • Make adjustments to the disciplinary process if needed (e.g., written warnings, additional time, support person)
  • Consider whether reasonable adjustments could prevent future conduct issues
  • Dismissal of a disabled employee requires careful consideration and usually occupational health advice

12. Ill Health and Return to Work

12.1 Supporting Return to Work

Reasonable adjustments are particularly important for employees returning from illness or injury:

  • Phased return (gradual increase in hours or duties)
  • Modified duties temporarily or permanently
  • Workplace or workstation adaptations
  • Ongoing occupational health support
  • Regular review meetings

12.2 Long-Term Health Conditions

  • Adjustments should be kept in place long-term if needed
  • Regular reviews to ensure adjustments remain effective
  • Support for fluctuating conditions
  • Consideration of redeployment if current role is no longer suitable

13. Manager Responsibilities

13.1 Creating an Inclusive Environment

Line managers play a key role in implementing this policy:

  • Be approachable and open to reasonable adjustment discussions
  • Respond promptly to requests for adjustments
  • Work with HR to assess and implement adjustments
  • Maintain confidentiality
  • Monitor effectiveness of adjustments
  • Challenge disability discrimination
  • Support team understanding (without breaching confidentiality)

13.2 Training

  • All managers receive disability awareness training
  • Training on reasonable adjustments and legal obligations
  • Guidance on having sensitive conversations
  • How to access support from HR and occupational health

14. Employee Responsibilities

14.1 Disclosure

  • While disclosure is voluntary, we can only make adjustments if we know about your needs
  • You are encouraged to disclose disabilities or health conditions so we can provide support
  • You can disclose at any time - during recruitment, on starting, or later during employment
  • Information will be handled confidentially

14.2 Engagement

  • Participate in discussions about adjustments
  • Provide relevant information about your needs
  • Be open about what works and what doesn't
  • Consent to medical assessments where appropriate
  • Try adjustments in good faith
  • Inform managers if circumstances change

15. Discrimination

15.1 Types of Disability Discrimination

The Equality Act protects against several types of discrimination:

  • Direct discrimination: Treating someone less favorably because of disability
  • Indirect discrimination: Policies or practices that disadvantage disabled people
  • Discrimination arising from disability: Treating someone unfavorably because of something connected to their disability
  • Failure to make reasonable adjustments: Not making changes that would remove disadvantages
  • Harassment: Unwanted conduct related to disability
  • Victimization: Treating someone badly because they've complained about discrimination

15.2 Zero Tolerance

  • TallTree Technologies has zero tolerance for disability discrimination
  • Discrimination by employees is a disciplinary matter
  • Harassment or bullying related to disability will result in disciplinary action
  • Reports of discrimination will be taken seriously and investigated

17. Examples of Reasonable Adjustments

Case Study Examples:

Example 1: Employee with anxiety disorder
An employee with anxiety finds team video calls overwhelming. Adjustments: Camera-off option for most meetings, one-to-one check-ins instead of group discussions, advance agendas, and written summaries afterwards.

Example 2: Employee with dyslexia
An employee with dyslexia struggles with written reports. Adjustments: Text-to-speech software, grammar checking tools, additional time for written work, ability to present findings verbally instead of written reports where appropriate.

Example 3: Employee with chronic fatigue syndrome
An employee with ME/CFS has limited energy. Adjustments: Reduced hours (80% contract), flexible start times, priority for core tasks, ability to take frequent short breaks, work from bed on difficult days with appropriate equipment setup.

Example 4: Employee with visual impairment
An employee has low vision. Adjustments: Screen reading software, high-contrast displays, documents in accessible formats, additional monitor with large display, good lighting setup.

Example 5: Employee with autism
An employee finds unpredictability difficult. Adjustments: Structured routine, advance notice of changes, clear written instructions, quiet workspace, predictable one-to-one meetings rather than spontaneous discussions.
