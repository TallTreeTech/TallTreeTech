Professional Malpractice Policy

Last updated: November 14, 2025

This policy establishes the framework for preventing, identifying, and addressing professional malpractice at Software Systems Limited, trading as TallTree Technologies.

1. Purpose and Scope

Objectives

This policy aims to:

  • Define professional standards expected of all personnel
  • Prevent professional negligence and malpractice
  • Establish procedures for identifying and managing malpractice risks
  • Ensure appropriate remediation when malpractice occurs
  • Protect clients, the company, and our professional reputation
  • Maintain compliance with professional and legal obligations

Application

This policy applies to:

  • All employees, contractors, and consultants
  • Directors and officers
  • Any person providing professional services on behalf of the company
  • All client engagements and professional services

2. Definition of Professional Malpractice

Professional malpractice includes any act or omission by a professional that falls below the accepted standard of practice and causes harm or loss to a client.

Examples of Malpractice

  • Professional negligence: Failure to exercise reasonable care and skill
  • Breach of duty: Not acting in client's best interests
  • Inadequate work: Deliverables not meeting specified requirements or standards
  • Missed deadlines: Failure to deliver within agreed timeframes without valid reason
  • Breach of confidentiality: Unauthorised disclosure of client information
  • Conflicts of interest: Unmanaged conflicts affecting objectivity
  • Misrepresentation: False claims about capabilities, credentials, or deliverables
  • Inadequate documentation: Failure to maintain proper records
  • Security failures: Inadequate protection of client data or systems
  • Non-compliance: Violation of applicable laws or regulations

3. Professional Standards Expected

All personnel must adhere to the following professional standards:

Competence

  • Possess appropriate qualifications, skills, and experience for assigned work
  • Only accept work within areas of competence
  • Maintain professional knowledge through continuing education
  • Seek expert assistance when needed
  • Decline engagements where competence cannot be assured

Due Care and Skill

  • Exercise reasonable care in all professional activities
  • Apply appropriate technical standards and methodologies
  • Thoroughly test and verify deliverables
  • Document work comprehensively
  • Review and validate assumptions and conclusions

Integrity and Honesty

  • Provide truthful and accurate information
  • Do not misrepresent capabilities or experience
  • Acknowledge limitations and uncertainties
  • Admit and correct errors promptly
  • Avoid misleading statements or omissions

Client Service

  • Act in clients' best interests (within legal and ethical bounds)
  • Communicate clearly and promptly
  • Manage client expectations realistically
  • Deliver on commitments
  • Respond to concerns and issues professionally

Confidentiality

  • Protect client confidential information
  • Use information only for authorised purposes
  • Implement appropriate security measures
  • Comply with NDAs and confidentiality obligations
  • Obtain consent before disclosure (except where legally required)

Independence and Objectivity

  • Identify and manage conflicts of interest
  • Provide unbiased professional judgment
  • Disclose relationships that may affect objectivity
  • Decline work where independence cannot be maintained

4. Conflicts of Interest

Identification

Conflicts of interest must be identified before accepting engagements and monitored throughout. Potential conflicts include:

  • Working for competing clients in same sector
  • Personal financial interests in client or competitors
  • Personal relationships affecting objectivity
  • Previous work creating conflicting obligations
  • Accepting gifts or benefits that may influence judgment

Disclosure

  • All potential conflicts must be disclosed immediately to management
  • Disclosure form completed for each new engagement
  • Annual declaration by all staff of outside interests and relationships

Management

When conflicts are identified:

  • Decline engagement: If conflict cannot be managed
  • Obtain consent: Disclose to affected parties and obtain informed consent
  • Information barriers: Implement Chinese walls between teams
  • Remove individual: Reassign personnel with conflict
  • Enhanced oversight: Additional review and monitoring

Gifts and Hospitality

  • Modest gifts (under £50) may be accepted if no obligation implied
  • Hospitality accepted only if reasonable and customary
  • Gifts/hospitality over £100 must be declared and approved
  • Cash gifts never accepted

5. Quality Assurance Procedures

Project Initiation

  • Clear scope definition and documentation
  • Competence assessment of assigned team
  • Risk assessment and mitigation planning
  • Conflicts of interest check
  • Quality plan development

Ongoing Quality Control

  • Project standards: Adherence to technical standards and methodologies
  • Documentation: Comprehensive records of decisions, assumptions, work performed
  • Testing: Thorough testing of all deliverables
  • Progress monitoring: Regular review against plan
  • Client communication: Regular updates and issue escalation

Deliverable Review

  • Peer review of all significant deliverables
  • Technical review by subject matter expert
  • Compliance check against requirements
  • Security review for technical implementations
  • Documentation completeness verification

Project Closeout

  • Client acceptance obtained and documented
  • Lessons learned documented
  • Knowledge transfer completed
  • Final documentation delivered
  • Post-implementation review scheduled

6. Peer Review Requirements

When Peer Review Required

Peer review is mandatory for:

  • All security implementations and assessments
  • Production system deployments
  • Architecture and design documents
  • Code for critical systems
  • Technical recommendations with significant impact
  • Projects over £25,000 value
  • High-risk or novel solutions

Peer Review Process

  • Reviewer selection: Peer with equivalent or greater expertise, not directly involved in work
  • Review scope: Technical adequacy, compliance with standards, completeness
  • Documentation: Review checklist completed and findings recorded
  • Resolution: Issues addressed before deliverable release
  • Sign-off: Reviewer approval documented

Technical Review Checklist

Reviewers should verify:

  • Requirements fully addressed
  • Technical approach sound and appropriate
  • Security considerations adequately addressed
  • Error handling and edge cases covered
  • Performance and scalability considered
  • Documentation clear and complete
  • Testing adequate and results satisfactory
  • Industry best practices followed

7. Professional Indemnity Insurance

Required Coverage

The company maintains Professional Indemnity Insurance with minimum coverage of:

  • Indemnity limit: £1,000,000 per claim
  • Aggregate limit: £2,000,000 per year
  • Run-off cover: Minimum 6 years

Policy Coverage

Insurance covers claims arising from:

  • Professional negligence
  • Errors and omissions
  • Breach of professional duty
  • Unintentional breach of confidentiality
  • Loss of client data or documents
  • Defamation (in professional capacity)

Exclusions

Typical policy exclusions include:

  • Intentional misconduct or fraud
  • Criminal acts
  • Contractual penalties and liquidated damages
  • Product liability (generally covered separately)
  • Cyber liability (separate cyber insurance maintained)

Claims Management

  • All potential claims reported to insurer immediately
  • Full cooperation with insurer's investigation
  • No admission of liability without insurer consent
  • Settlement negotiations conducted with insurer involvement

8. Incident Reporting

Mandatory Reporting

All personnel must immediately report any incident that may constitute professional malpractice or that could lead to a malpractice claim.

Reportable Incidents

  • Errors or defects in delivered work
  • Security breaches or data loss
  • Missed deadlines with potential client impact
  • Client complaints about quality or conduct
  • Breaches of confidentiality
  • Conflicts of interest discovered after engagement commenced
  • Non-compliance with legal or regulatory requirements
  • Threats of legal action
  • Any situation where client may suffer loss due to our actions/omissions

How to Report

  • Immediate verbal notification: To line manager and malpractice@talltree.tech
  • Written report within 24 hours: Using incident report form
  • Confidential reporting: Available via confidential@talltree.tech if concerns about reporting through normal channels

Information to Include

  • Description of the incident
  • When it occurred and when discovered
  • Client(s) and project(s) affected
  • Potential or actual impact
  • Actions taken to date
  • Individuals involved
  • Supporting documentation

No Retaliation

The company prohibits retaliation against anyone who reports malpractice concerns in good faith. Whistleblower protections apply.

9. Investigation Procedures

Initial Assessment (Within 24 hours)

  • Senior management notified
  • Preliminary assessment of severity and risk
  • Immediate containment actions taken
  • Professional indemnity insurer notified if potential claim
  • Legal counsel consulted if appropriate

Investigation Team

Appointed based on incident severity:

  • Minor incidents: Line manager investigation
  • Moderate incidents: Senior management investigation
  • Serious incidents: Director-led investigation, may include external experts

Investigation Process

  • Evidence gathering: Review documents, communications, work products, system logs
  • Interviews: Speak with involved personnel, witnesses, client if appropriate
  • Technical analysis: Expert review of technical work
  • Standards comparison: Assess against professional standards and contract requirements
  • Causation analysis: Identify root causes and contributing factors

Investigation Timeline

  • Initial findings within 7 days
  • Full investigation report within 21 days (or sooner if urgent)
  • Extensions documented with justification

Investigation Documentation

  • Terms of reference
  • Evidence collected
  • Interview notes
  • Analysis and findings
  • Conclusions on whether malpractice occurred
  • Recommendations for corrective action

10. Corrective Actions

Remediation of Client Impact

When malpractice has occurred, immediate steps to address client impact:

  • Correct errors or defects at no additional cost
  • Provide additional resources to meet deadlines
  • Engage alternative experts if needed
  • Compensate for demonstrable losses (in consultation with insurer)
  • Offer remedies per contract terms or as appropriate

Personnel Actions

Depending on nature and severity of malpractice:

  • Coaching and training: For inadvertent errors or knowledge gaps
  • Formal warning: For negligence or repeated issues
  • Reassignment: Remove from client work or specific types of work
  • Supervision requirements: Enhanced oversight of work
  • Suspension: Pending investigation of serious incidents
  • Termination: For gross negligence, intentional misconduct, or repeated malpractice

Process Improvements

  • Identify systemic issues and process weaknesses
  • Implement enhanced quality controls
  • Update policies, procedures, or standards
  • Provide additional training to affected personnel or wider team
  • Enhance supervision or peer review requirements

Technology and Tool Improvements

  • Implement additional testing or validation tools
  • Enhance security controls
  • Improve documentation templates or systems
  • Deploy automated quality checks

11. Client Notification Requirements

When to Notify Clients

Clients must be notified promptly when:

  • Malpractice has resulted in defective deliverables or services
  • Client data has been compromised
  • Security vulnerabilities have been introduced
  • Client may suffer loss or damage due to our error
  • Remedial action is required from client
  • Contractual obligations have been breached

Notification Process

  • Approval: Director approval required before client notification
  • Legal review: Notification reviewed by legal counsel
  • Insurer consultation: Coordinate with professional indemnity insurer
  • Timing: As soon as practical after incident confirmed
  • Method: Written notification to client senior contact

Notification Content

  • Description of the incident (factual, avoid speculation)
  • Impact on client
  • Actions taken to address the issue
  • Proposed remediation
  • Steps to prevent recurrence
  • Contact information for questions

What Not to Include

  • Admission of legal liability (without insurer/legal advice)
  • Speculation about causes
  • Information about internal personnel actions
  • Excessive technical detail not relevant to client

12. Record Keeping

Malpractice Incident Register

Centralized register maintained with:

  • Incident reference number
  • Date reported and date occurred
  • Type of incident
  • Client and project affected
  • Personnel involved
  • Investigation summary
  • Findings and conclusions
  • Corrective actions taken
  • Client notification (if applicable)
  • Insurance claim (if applicable)
  • Final resolution

Investigation Files

Maintained for each incident:

  • Initial incident report
  • Investigation plan and terms of reference
  • Evidence collected
  • Interview records
  • Technical analysis
  • Investigation report and findings
  • Corrective action plan
  • Client correspondence
  • Insurance correspondence

Retention Period

Malpractice records retained for 7 years minimum

Longer retention if:

  • Legal proceedings ongoing or anticipated
  • Insurance claim pending
  • Regulatory investigation

Confidentiality and Security

  • Malpractice records treated as highly confidential
  • Access restricted to authorised personnel only
  • Stored securely with encryption
  • Personal data handled in accordance with GDPR

13. Continuous Improvement

Trend Analysis

  • Quarterly review of all malpractice incidents
  • Identify patterns and trends
  • Root cause analysis of recurring issues
  • Benchmarking against industry data

Lessons Learned

  • Extract lessons from each significant incident
  • Share learning across organisation (anonymised)
  • Update training materials
  • Revise procedures and controls

Preventive Actions

  • Proactive identification of malpractice risks
  • Implementation of controls before issues occur
  • Regular competency assessments
  • Enhanced training programs

Performance Metrics

Track and monitor:

  • Number of malpractice incidents (by type and severity)
  • Time to detect incidents
  • Time to resolve incidents
  • Client satisfaction after incident resolution
  • Effectiveness of corrective actions (recurrence rates)
  • Cost of malpractice (remediation, insurance claims)

Annual Review

  • Comprehensive annual review of malpractice prevention program
  • Assessment of policy effectiveness
  • Update policies and procedures
  • Review insurance coverage adequacy
  • Report to Board on malpractice risks and incidents
