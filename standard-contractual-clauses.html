Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs)

For International Data Transfers from the UK/EEA

EU Commission Implementing Decision 2021/914 - Module 2: Controller to Processor

Critical Notice - UK to EU/EEA Data Transfers Post-Brexit:

Following the UK's exit from the European Union, personal data transfers from the UK to the EU/EEA (and vice versa) are now considered international data transfers that require appropriate safeguards under UK GDPR and EU GDPR.

When these SCCs are required:

  • UK to EU/EEA transfers: Required when TallTree (UK-based) processes personal data on behalf of EU/EEA-based clients
  • EU/EEA to UK transfers: May be required until UK adequacy decision is renewed or if EU adequacy for UK is withdrawn
  • UK to third countries: Required for transfers to countries without UK adequacy decisions
  • EU to third countries: Required for transfers to countries without EU adequacy decisions

Alternative for UK transfers: The UK International Data Transfer Agreement (IDTA) or International Data Transfer Addendum to EU SCCs can be used for UK-specific transfers. See Section 10 for UK IDTA option.

Purpose of This Document

These Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) provide appropriate safeguards for international transfers of personal data, as required by:

  • Article 46 of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (EU GDPR)
  • Article 46 of the UK GDPR (as incorporated by the Data Protection Act 2018)

Module 2 Configuration: This document uses Module 2 (Controller to Processor), which is appropriate for consulting engagements where:

  • The Client acts as a data controller (determines purposes and means of processing)
  • TallTree acts as a data processor (processes personal data on behalf of the Client)

Section 3: Standard Contractual Clauses (Module 2: Controller to Processor)

Clause 1: Purpose and Scope

  1. The purpose of these standard contractual clauses is to ensure compliance with the requirements of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 27 April 2016 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data (General Data Protection Regulation) for the transfer of personal data to a third country.
  2. The Parties:
    1. the natural or legal person(s), public authority/ies, agency/ies or other body/ies (hereinafter 'entity/ies') transferring the personal data, as listed in Annex I.A (hereinafter each 'data exporter'), and
    2. the entity/ies in a third country receiving the personal data from the data exporter, directly or indirectly via another entity also Party to these Clauses, as listed in Annex I.A (hereinafter each 'data importer')
    have agreed to these standard contractual clauses (hereinafter: 'Clauses').
  3. These Clauses apply with respect to the transfer of personal data as specified in Annex I.B.
  4. The Appendix to these Clauses containing the Annexes referred to therein forms an integral part of these Clauses.

Clause 2: Effect and Invariability of the Clauses

  1. These Clauses set out appropriate safeguards, including enforceable data subject rights and effective legal remedies, pursuant to Article 46(1) and Article 46(2)(c) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and, with respect to data transfers from controllers to processors and/or processors to processors, standard contractual clauses pursuant to Article 28(7) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679, provided they are not modified, except to select the appropriate Module(s) or to add or update information in the Appendix.
  2. These Clauses are without prejudice to obligations to which the data exporter is subject by virtue of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.

Clause 3: Third-Party Beneficiaries

  1. Data subjects may invoke and enforce these Clauses, as third-party beneficiaries, against the data exporter and/or data importer, with the following exceptions:
  2. Clause 1, Clause 2, Clause 3, Clause 6, Clause 7;
  3. Clause 8.1(b), 8.9(a), (c), (d) and (e);
  4. Clause 9(a), (c), (d) and (e);
  5. Clause 12(a), (d) and (f);
  6. Clause 13;
  7. Clause 15.1(c), (d) and (e);
  8. Clause 16(e);
  9. Clause 18(a) and (b).

Clause 4: Interpretation

  1. Where these Clauses use terms that are defined in Regulation (EU) 2016/679, those terms shall have the same meaning as in that Regulation.
  2. These Clauses shall be read and interpreted in the light of the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.
  3. These Clauses shall not be interpreted in a way that conflicts with rights and obligations provided for in Regulation (EU) 2016/679.

Clause 5: Hierarchy

In the event of a contradiction between these Clauses and the provisions of related agreements between the Parties, existing at the time these Clauses are agreed or entered into thereafter, these Clauses shall prevail.

Clause 6: Description of the Transfer(s)

The details of the transfer(s), and in particular the categories of personal data that are transferred and the purpose(s) for which they are transferred, are specified in Annex I.B.

Clause 7: Docking Clause

  1. An entity that is not a Party to these Clauses may, with the agreement of the Parties, accede to these Clauses at any time, either as a data exporter or as a data importer, by completing the Appendix and signing Annex I.A.
  2. Once it has completed the Appendix and signed Annex I.A, the acceding entity shall become a Party to these Clauses and have the rights and obligations of a data exporter or data importer in accordance with its designation in Annex I.A.
  3. The acceding entity shall have no rights or obligations arising under these Clauses from the period prior to becoming a Party.

Section 4: Key Processor Obligations (Summary)

Data Processing (Clause 8)

The data importer (TallTree) shall:

  • Process personal data only on documented instructions from the data exporter (Client)
  • Ensure persons authorized to process personal data are subject to confidentiality obligations
  • Take appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure security (see Annex II)
  • Respect conditions for engaging sub-processors (see Annex III)
  • Assist the data exporter in responding to data subject requests
  • Assist the data exporter in ensuring compliance with security, breach notification, and impact assessment obligations
  • Delete or return all personal data after the end of services (at data exporter's choice)
  • Make available information necessary to demonstrate compliance

Sub-Processors (Clause 9)

The data importer shall:

  • Not engage sub-processors without prior specific or general written authorization from the data exporter
  • Where general authorization is given, inform the data exporter of any changes and allow objection
  • Ensure sub-processors are bound by the same data protection obligations via contract
  • Remain fully liable to the data exporter for performance of sub-processor obligations

Current authorized sub-processors are listed in Annex III.

Data Subject Rights (Clause 10)

The data importer shall promptly inform the data exporter of any request received directly from a data subject and shall not respond to the request unless authorized to do so by the data exporter.

Government Access Requests (Clause 15)

The data importer shall:

  • Notify the data exporter and data subjects (unless prohibited by law) of any legally binding request for disclosure by a public authority
  • Challenge the request if it believes there are reasonable grounds to consider it unlawful
  • Provide minimum amount of information permissible when responding to the request
  • Document all requests and responses and make information available to the data exporter

Annex II: Technical and Organizational Measures (TOMs)

The data importer has implemented and will maintain the following technical and organizational measures to ensure an appropriate level of security:

1. Access Control

  • Multi-factor authentication (MFA) for all system access
  • Role-based access control (RBAC) with principle of least privilege
  • Regular access reviews and revocation of unnecessary permissions
  • Secure authentication mechanisms and password policies
  • Logging and monitoring of all access to personal data

2. Transmission Control

  • Encryption of data in transit using TLS 1.3 or higher
  • Secure file transfer protocols (SFTP, HTTPS)
  • VPN for remote access where applicable
  • Logging of all data transmissions

3. Storage and Isolation Control

  • Encryption of data at rest using AES-256 or equivalent
  • Logical separation of client data (multi-tenancy security)
  • Secure backup and recovery procedures
  • Data retention and secure deletion policies

4. Physical Security

  • Access controls to office premises (key card, biometric)
  • Secure areas for processing sensitive data
  • Clean desk and clear screen policies
  • Secure disposal of physical media (shredding, degaussing)

5. Organizational Measures

  • Data protection and security policies and procedures
  • Confidentiality agreements for all personnel
  • Regular security awareness training
  • Background checks for employees with access to personal data
  • Incident response and breach notification procedures
  • Regular security assessments and penetration testing

6. Development and Maintenance

  • Security by design and by default principles
  • Secure development lifecycle (SDLC) processes
  • Regular security patching and updates
  • Vulnerability management program
  • Code review and security testing

7. Incident Response

  • 24/7 security monitoring and alerting
  • Documented incident response procedures
  • Breach notification procedures (72-hour notification to data exporter)
  • Forensic investigation capabilities
  • Post-incident review and remediation

8. Business Continuity

  • Regular data backups with encryption
  • Disaster recovery plan tested annually
  • Business continuity procedures
  • Redundant systems and failover capabilities

Section 6: Data Exporter's Rights

Audit Rights

The data exporter (or an independent auditor mandated by the data exporter) may conduct audits, including inspections, to verify compliance with these Clauses. Such audits shall be conducted:

  • Upon reasonable notice (at least 30 days)
  • During normal business hours
  • No more than once per year (unless required by supervisory authority or following a data breach)
  • Subject to confidentiality obligations
  • At data exporter's expense (unless non-compliance is found)

Right to Suspend or Terminate

The data exporter may suspend the transfer of personal data and/or terminate these Clauses if:

  • The data importer is in substantial or persistent breach of these Clauses
  • The data importer is unable to comply with these Clauses due to legal obligations
  • A competent supervisory authority requires suspension or termination

Section 7: Liability and Indemnification

Liability (Clause 12)

  • Each Party is liable to the other Party for damages caused by breach of these Clauses
  • Data subjects may claim compensation from either the data exporter or data importer for material or non-material damage
  • If both Parties are responsible for the damage, they are jointly and severally liable
  • A Party is exempt from liability if it proves it is not responsible for the breach

Indemnification

The data importer shall indemnify the data exporter for any costs, claims, damages, or expenses arising from breach of these Clauses by the data importer or its sub-processors.

Section 10: UK International Data Transfer Agreement (IDTA) Alternative

UK IDTA Option

As an alternative to these EU SCCs for UK-specific data transfers, the parties may elect to use the UK International Data Transfer Agreement (IDTA) approved by the ICO, or the International Data Transfer Addendum to the EU SCCs.

When to use UK IDTA:

  • When the data exporter is subject to UK GDPR (established in the UK or targeting UK data subjects)
  • When transferring personal data from the UK to any jurisdiction outside the UK (including EU/EEA)
  • When UK adequacy regulations do not apply to the transfer

IDTA Components:

  • Part 1: Tables (parties, details, audits)
  • Part 2: Mandatory Clauses (21 clauses covering obligations, warranties, rights, liability)
  • Annexes: Similar to SCCs (details of transfer, security measures, sub-processors)

Important Implementation Notes

  • Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA): Before implementing these SCCs, the data exporter should conduct a Transfer Impact Assessment to evaluate whether the SCCs provide adequate protection in light of the data importer's jurisdiction and local laws.
  • Supplementary Measures: If the TIA identifies risks, consider implementing supplementary technical or organizational measures (e.g., encryption, pseudonymization, data minimization).
  • Documentation: Maintain records of the TIA, SCCs, and any supplementary measures as part of your accountability obligations under Article 5(2) GDPR.
  • Review: Review these SCCs and the adequacy of protections at least annually or when circumstances change.
  • Integration: These SCCs should be read together with the Master Services Agreement, Data Processing Agreement, and relevant Statement(s) of Work.
