Health and Safety Policy

For Remote Workers

Last updated: November 14, 2025

Policy Statement: TallTree Technologies is committed to ensuring the health, safety, and welfare of all employees working remotely. This policy outlines our approach to managing health and safety risks for home-based workers in compliance with the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and associated regulations.

1. Legal Framework

This policy is based on and complies with the following legislation:

  • Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974
  • Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999
  • Health and Safety (Display Screen Equipment) Regulations 1992
  • Workplace (Health, Safety and Welfare) Regulations 1992
  • Provision and Use of Work Equipment Regulations 1998
  • The Working Time Regulations 1998
  • Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 (RIDDOR)

2. Scope

This policy applies to all TallTree Technologies employees working from home or other remote locations. The company's health and safety duties extend to all employees regardless of their work location.

3. Policy Objectives

  • Prevent work-related accidents and ill health
  • Provide safe equipment and systems of work
  • Ensure employees have appropriate information, instruction, and training
  • Maintain a culture of safety awareness and continuous improvement
  • Comply with all relevant health and safety legislation
  • Support physical and mental wellbeing of remote workers

4. Employer Responsibilities

TallTree Technologies, as your employer, has a duty to protect your health and safety while working from home. We will:

4.1 Risk Assessment

  • Assess the risks associated with home working activities
  • Identify control measures to minimize those risks
  • Review and update risk assessments regularly
  • Provide guidance on conducting home workspace assessments

4.2 Equipment and Resources

  • Provide appropriate equipment necessary for safe home working
  • Ensure all equipment meets relevant safety standards
  • Arrange for regular equipment maintenance and PAT testing where required
  • Provide DSE equipment following workstation assessments
  • Replace faulty or damaged equipment promptly

4.3 Information and Training

  • Provide health and safety induction training for new employees
  • Offer guidance on safe home working practices
  • Provide DSE awareness training
  • Communicate health and safety updates and best practices
  • Offer mental health and wellbeing resources

4.4 Support and Monitoring

  • Maintain regular contact with remote workers
  • Conduct regular one-to-one meetings to discuss wellbeing
  • Respond promptly to health and safety concerns
  • Investigate accidents, incidents, and near-misses
  • Monitor working patterns to prevent excessive hours

4.5 Insurance

  • Maintain appropriate employer's liability insurance
  • Ensure coverage extends to home working arrangements
  • Review insurance provisions regularly

5. Employee Responsibilities

All employees have a legal duty to take reasonable care of their own health and safety and that of others who may be affected by their actions. You must:

5.1 General Duties

  • Follow the health and safety policies and procedures
  • Take reasonable care of your own health, safety, and wellbeing
  • Use equipment safely and as instructed
  • Report hazards, accidents, incidents, and near-misses promptly
  • Not put yourself or others at risk
  • Cooperate with the company on health and safety matters
  • Attend required health and safety training

5.2 Home Workspace

  • Maintain a safe and suitable home working environment
  • Complete a DSE self-assessment and implement recommendations
  • Ensure your workspace has adequate lighting, heating, and ventilation
  • Keep work areas tidy and free from trip hazards
  • Ensure electrical installations are safe
  • Inform your home insurer that you work from home

5.3 Work Practices

  • Take regular breaks from screen work
  • Maintain good posture and ergonomic practices
  • Follow safe working time patterns
  • Manage your workload effectively
  • Seek support if feeling overwhelmed or stressed

6. Display Screen Equipment (DSE) Assessments

6.1 Legal Requirements

Under the Health and Safety (Display Screen Equipment) Regulations 1992, employers must assess workstations used by employees who regularly use DSE for an hour or more at a time.

6.2 DSE Self-Assessment

All employees must complete a DSE self-assessment:

  • Within the first month of employment
  • Annually thereafter
  • When changing work location
  • Following any changes to equipment
  • If experiencing discomfort or health issues

6.3 Assessment Process

  1. Complete the online DSE self-assessment form
  2. Review the automated recommendations
  3. Implement changes to your workstation setup
  4. Request equipment or support if needed
  5. Submit completed assessment to your line manager

6.4 Follow-Up Actions

  • The company will review all DSE assessments
  • Required equipment or adjustments will be provided within 4 weeks
  • If significant issues are identified, a remote or in-person assessment may be arranged
  • Occupational health referral may be offered where appropriate

7. Home Workstation Setup Guidance

7.1 Desk and Chair

  • Desk: Stable, adequate size for equipment, appropriate height (typically 70-75cm)
  • Chair: Adjustable height, supportive backrest, stable base with castors
  • Position: Chair height adjusted so feet flat on floor and thighs horizontal
  • Support: Lower back should be supported by the chair backrest

7.2 Monitor Setup

  • Height: Top of screen at or slightly below eye level
  • Distance: Arm's length away (50-70cm)
  • Position: Directly in front of you, not twisted to side
  • Brightness: Adjusted to match lighting conditions
  • Reflection: Screen positioned to minimize glare and reflections

7.3 Keyboard and Mouse

  • Keyboard: Directly in front of you, 10-15cm from desk edge
  • Mouse: Close to keyboard, within easy reach
  • Wrists: Straight and supported when typing
  • Arms: Relaxed at sides, elbows at roughly 90 degrees

7.4 Lighting

  • Adequate lighting to read documents and see the screen
  • Natural light should be to the side of the screen, not behind or in front
  • Use blinds or curtains to control natural light
  • Task lighting may be beneficial for reading documents
  • Avoid working in dim lighting or with excessive brightness

7.5 Working Environment

  • Temperature: Comfortable temperature, typically 16-24°C
  • Ventilation: Fresh air circulation, avoid stuffy or drafty conditions
  • Noise: Minimize background noise where possible
  • Space: Sufficient room to move and change position
  • Privacy: Ability to conduct confidential work and calls

8. Breaks and Working Time Regulations

8.1 Working Time Regulations

Under the Working Time Regulations 1998, employees are entitled to:

  • Maximum 48-hour average working week (unless opted out)
  • Minimum 11 consecutive hours rest in each 24-hour period
  • Minimum uninterrupted rest period of 24 hours in each 7-day period
  • Minimum 20-minute rest break if working more than 6 hours

8.2 DSE Break Requirements

When working with display screen equipment, you should:

  • Take a 5-10 minute break from DSE work every hour
  • Breaks should involve moving away from the screen
  • Short, frequent breaks are more effective than longer, less frequent ones
  • Mix screen-based and non-screen tasks where possible
  • Look away from the screen regularly (every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds)

8.3 Lunch Breaks

  • Take a proper lunch break away from your workstation
  • Minimum 30-minute unpaid break
  • Use this time to rest, eat, and recharge
  • Avoid working through lunch regularly

8.4 Preventing Overwork

  • Establish clear boundaries between work and personal time
  • Log off at the end of your working day
  • Avoid checking emails outside working hours
  • Speak to your manager if your workload is unmanageable
  • The company does not expect employees to work excessive hours

9. Mental Health and Wellbeing

9.1 Recognizing Mental Health Risks

Remote working can present specific mental health challenges:

  • Social isolation and loneliness
  • Blurred work-life boundaries
  • Lack of separation between work and home
  • Communication challenges
  • Increased stress or anxiety

9.2 Supporting Mental Wellbeing

TallTree Technologies supports employee mental health through:

  • Regular check-ins and one-to-one meetings
  • Open-door policy for raising concerns
  • Mental health awareness training for managers
  • Flexible working arrangements where appropriate
  • Access to mental health resources and support
  • Employee Assistance Programme (if available)

9.3 Self-Care Strategies

Employees are encouraged to:

  • Maintain regular working hours and routines
  • Take regular breaks and use annual leave
  • Stay connected with colleagues through virtual coffee breaks
  • Exercise regularly and maintain physical health
  • Create physical separation between work and personal space where possible
  • Practice stress management techniques
  • Seek support early if experiencing difficulties

9.4 Getting Help

If you're struggling with mental health or wellbeing:

  • Speak to your line manager or HR
  • Request a wellbeing conversation or occupational health referral
  • Contact your GP or mental health services
  • Use the Employee Assistance Programme (if available)
  • Call Samaritans (116 123) or other helplines for immediate support

10. Incident Reporting

10.1 What to Report

You must report:

  • Accidents: Any work-related injury or ill health
  • Near-misses: Incidents that could have caused harm
  • Dangerous occurrences: Serious incidents such as equipment failure
  • Work-related ill health: Including repetitive strain injuries, stress
  • Equipment defects: Any faulty or dangerous equipment
  • Hazards: Anything that could cause harm

10.2 Reporting Process

  1. For serious injuries requiring emergency treatment, call 999 first
  2. Report the incident to your line manager immediately (same day where possible)
  3. Complete an incident report form within 24 hours
  4. Provide details of: what happened, when, where, who was involved, witnesses, injuries, treatment
  5. Keep any evidence (photos, damaged equipment, etc.)
  6. Cooperate with any investigation

10.3 Investigation

  • All reportable incidents will be investigated
  • The purpose is to understand causes and prevent recurrence, not to apportion blame
  • Findings and actions will be documented
  • Lessons learned will be shared to improve safety

10.4 RIDDOR Reporting

Certain serious incidents must be reported to the Health and Safety Executive under RIDDOR:

  • Work-related deaths
  • Specified serious injuries
  • Injuries resulting in more than 7 days' absence
  • Diagnosed occupational diseases
  • Dangerous occurrences

HR will handle RIDDOR reporting where required.

11. First Aid (Remote Context)

11.1 First Aid at Home

When working from home:

  • Ensure you have access to a basic first aid kit
  • Know how to contact emergency services (999)
  • If injured, prioritize getting appropriate treatment
  • Inform your manager after dealing with any immediate medical needs

11.2 Emergency Contacts

  • Emergency services: 999
  • NHS non-emergency: 111
  • Line manager: [Contact details provided individually]
  • HR: hr@talltree.tech

11.3 Medical Emergencies During Work

If you experience a medical emergency while working:

  1. Call 999 immediately if needed
  2. Inform a colleague or manager if possible (via phone or Slack)
  3. Seek appropriate medical treatment
  4. Report the incident to your manager when safe to do so
  5. Complete an incident report form

12.1 General Risk Assessment

TallTree Technologies conducts risk assessments for home working covering:

  • Workstation setup and DSE use
  • Electrical safety
  • Fire safety
  • Manual handling (equipment setup/movement)
  • Lone working
  • Work-related stress
  • Slips, trips, and falls

12.2 Individual Risk Assessment

Individual risk assessments may be required for:

  • New or expectant mothers
  • Young workers
  • Disabled employees or those with health conditions
  • Employees returning from long-term absence
  • Specific high-risk activities

12.3 Risk Assessment Review

  • General risk assessments reviewed annually
  • Individual assessments reviewed when circumstances change
  • Following any incidents or near-misses
  • When new hazards are identified

13. Specific Hazards and Controls

13.1 Electrical Safety

  • Visually inspect equipment and cables regularly
  • Report any damage, exposed wires, or burning smells immediately
  • Don't overload sockets (use one plug per socket where possible)
  • Don't use damaged equipment
  • Ensure proper ventilation around equipment to prevent overheating
  • Company will arrange PAT testing of portable equipment periodically

13.2 Fire Safety

  • Ensure working smoke alarms in your home
  • Know your escape routes from your workspace
  • Keep escape routes clear
  • Don't block radiators or heaters with equipment
  • Switch off equipment when not in use
  • Have a fire extinguisher and know how to use it (recommended)

13.3 Manual Handling

  • Avoid lifting heavy items alone
  • Use proper lifting technique (bend knees, keep back straight)
  • Request delivery and setup assistance for heavy equipment
  • Take care when moving furniture or equipment

13.4 Slips, Trips, and Falls

  • Keep work areas tidy
  • Secure trailing cables
  • Clean up spills immediately
  • Ensure adequate lighting
  • Wear appropriate footwear

13.5 Lone Working

  • Maintain regular contact with colleagues and managers
  • Ensure someone knows your working hours
  • Have a means of raising the alarm in an emergency
  • Don't put yourself at risk
  • Report concerns about lone working to your manager
