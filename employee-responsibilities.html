Employee Responsibilities

Software Systems Limited trading as TallTree Technologies

Last updated: November 14, 2025

Purpose: This policy outlines the core responsibilities and expected standards of conduct for all employees of the Company. These responsibilities ensure professional service delivery, legal compliance, and a positive working environment.

1. Overview

As an employee of the Company, you have responsibilities to:

  • The Company - delivering quality work and upholding our reputation
  • Our clients - providing professional and ethical service
  • Your colleagues - fostering a collaborative and respectful environment
  • Yourself - maintaining professional development and wellbeing

2. Professional Conduct

2.1 Standards of Behaviour

All employees are expected to:

Professionalism

  • Conduct yourself professionally at all times
  • Represent the Company positively to clients and stakeholders
  • Maintain high ethical standards in all business dealings
  • Act with integrity, honesty, and transparency
  • Declare any conflicts of interest promptly

Respect and Dignity

  • Treat all colleagues, clients, and partners with respect and courtesy
  • Value diversity and promote an inclusive environment
  • Refrain from discrimination, harassment, or bullying
  • Listen actively and communicate respectfully
  • Support colleagues and collaborate effectively

Client Service

  • Provide high-quality, timely service to all clients
  • Respond to client communications promptly (within 24 hours for emails)
  • Escalate issues or concerns appropriately
  • Maintain professional boundaries
  • Act in the client's best interests (within scope of engagement)

2.2 Business Ethics

Anti-Bribery and Corruption

  • Comply with the Bribery Act 2010
  • Never offer, accept, or solicit bribes or improper payments
  • Exercise caution with gifts and hospitality (see policy limits)
  • Report any suspected bribery or corruption

Conflicts of Interest

  • Disclose any actual or potential conflicts of interest
  • Seek approval before engaging in outside employment or business
  • Do not use Company resources for personal gain
  • Avoid situations that could compromise objectivity

Financial Integrity

  • Submit accurate and honest expense claims
  • Comply with financial controls and approval processes
  • Report any suspected fraud or financial irregularity
  • Never falsify records or documents

2.3 Gifts and Hospitality

Employees may accept or offer gifts and hospitality within reasonable limits:

  • Acceptable: Modest gifts (under £50), occasional meals, standard corporate hospitality
  • Not acceptable: Cash or cash equivalents, excessive gifts, anything that could influence business decisions
  • Disclosure: Register all gifts/hospitality over £25 with line manager
  • When in doubt: Decline politely or seek guidance from HR

3. Confidentiality Obligations

3.1 Confidential Information

Employees must protect confidential information belonging to the Company and our clients:

Company Confidential Information Includes:

  • Business strategies, plans, and financial information
  • Client lists and contact details
  • Commercial terms, pricing, and contracts
  • Intellectual property and proprietary methodologies
  • Internal communications and discussions
  • Employee information and HR matters
  • Trade secrets and know-how

Client Confidential Information Includes:

  • All information disclosed by clients in the course of engagement
  • Business operations, strategies, and plans
  • Technical information and data
  • Personal data of client employees or customers
  • Any information marked as confidential
  • Information that a reasonable person would consider confidential

3.2 Confidentiality Responsibilities

You must:

  • Keep information secure: Use appropriate security measures (encryption, access controls, etc.)
  • Share on need-to-know basis: Only with authorized individuals for legitimate business purposes
  • Do not disclose externally: To competitors, media, or unauthorized third parties
  • Be cautious in public: Do not discuss confidential matters in public places or on social media
  • Protect after employment: Confidentiality obligations continue after leaving the Company
  • Return information: Return or securely destroy all confidential information when leaving

3.3 Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs)

Employees may be required to sign:

  • Company NDA (standard in employment contract)
  • Client-specific NDAs for particular projects
  • Third-party NDAs when accessing partner or vendor information

Comply with all NDA terms and seek clarification if uncertain about obligations.

3.4 Exceptions to Confidentiality

Confidentiality obligations do not prevent:

  • Disclosures required by law or court order
  • Protected disclosures under whistleblowing legislation
  • Reporting of suspected criminal activity
  • Cooperation with regulatory authorities

See the Company's Whistleblowing Policy for guidance on protected disclosures.

4. Data Protection Compliance

4.1 UK GDPR Responsibilities

All employees must comply with UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018:

Personal Data Handling

  • Only process personal data when necessary for your role
  • Process data fairly, lawfully, and transparently
  • Collect only the minimum data needed
  • Keep data accurate and up to date
  • Store data securely and delete when no longer needed
  • Respect data subject rights (access, rectification, erasure, etc.)

What is Personal Data?

  • Any information relating to an identified or identifiable person
  • Examples: Names, email addresses, phone numbers, IP addresses, employee records
  • Special category data: Health, ethnicity, religion, biometrics - requires extra care

4.2 Data Security Requirements

You must:

  • Use strong passwords: Minimum 12 characters, unique passwords, use password manager
  • Enable MFA: Multi-factor authentication on all systems
  • Encrypt data: Use encryption for data in transit and at rest
  • Lock devices: Lock screen when away from device
  • Use secure channels: Company-approved tools for sharing sensitive data
  • Avoid public Wi-Fi: Use VPN if accessing company data on public networks
  • Clean desk policy: Do not leave confidential documents or devices unsecured
  • Secure disposal: Shred physical documents, securely wipe digital data

4.3 Data Breach Response

If you suspect a data breach (loss, unauthorized access, or disclosure of personal data):

  1. Report immediately: Email security@talltree.tech within 24 hours
  2. Contain the breach: Take immediate steps to prevent further loss
  3. Preserve evidence: Do not delete logs or evidence
  4. Cooperate with investigation: Provide full information to investigators
Critical: Data breaches must be reported to the ICO within 72 hours. Early internal reporting is essential.

4.4 Email and Communication Security

  • Verify recipients: Double-check email addresses before sending sensitive information
  • Use BCC: When emailing multiple external recipients
  • Encrypted email: Use encrypted email for sensitive personal data
  • Phishing awareness: Be vigilant for phishing attempts, verify sender before clicking links or attachments
  • Report suspicious emails: Forward to security@talltree.tech

See the Company's Data Protection Policy for comprehensive requirements.

5. Security Responsibilities

5.1 Information Security

System Access and Authentication

  • Keep login credentials confidential - never share passwords
  • Use password manager for secure password storage
  • Change passwords immediately if compromised
  • Log out when finished or lock screen when away
  • Do not allow others to use your account

Device Security

  • Keep all devices (laptops, phones, tablets) physically secure
  • Enable full-disk encryption on all devices
  • Install security updates and patches promptly
  • Use company-provided antivirus and security software
  • Report lost or stolen devices immediately to IT
  • Do not install unauthorized software

Remote Working Security

  • Ensure home office is secure and private
  • Use company VPN when accessing company resources remotely
  • Position screens away from windows/public view
  • Ensure family members cannot access work devices
  • Be cautious when working in public spaces (cafes, co-working spaces)

5.2 Acceptable Use of IT Systems

Company IT systems and equipment must be used appropriately:

Acceptable Use

  • Business purposes and reasonable personal use
  • Professional and lawful activities
  • In accordance with Company policies

Prohibited Use

  • Illegal activities or accessing illegal content
  • Downloading or distributing pirated software, media, or content
  • Accessing inappropriate or offensive material
  • Harassment, discrimination, or bullying
  • Unauthorized sharing of confidential information
  • Activities that could damage Company reputation
  • Personal commercial activities or unauthorized business use
  • Circumventing security controls
  • Excessive personal use that affects productivity

5.3 Monitoring and Privacy

Notice: The Company reserves the right to monitor use of IT systems to:

  • Ensure compliance with policies
  • Detect and prevent security incidents
  • Protect Company interests
  • Comply with legal obligations

Monitoring is conducted in accordance with UK GDPR and employee privacy rights. Personal use should not be assumed to be private.

5.4 Social Media and Online Conduct

When using social media (professionally or personally):

  • Be professional: Ensure content reflects well on you and the Company
  • Respect confidentiality: Do not disclose confidential Company or client information
  • Clarify personal views: Make clear when expressing personal opinions (not Company views)
  • Be respectful: Do not post offensive, discriminatory, or defamatory content
  • Protect reputation: Avoid conduct that could damage Company reputation
  • Client confidentiality: Do not disclose client engagements without permission
  • Seek approval: For speaking on behalf of the Company or about Company matters

6. Remote Working Obligations

6.1 Home Office Standards

When working remotely, you must:

  • Suitable workspace: Maintain a safe, secure, and productive work environment
  • Health and safety: Conduct self-assessment of home office (DSE assessment)
  • Insurance: Notify home insurer of regular home working (if required by policy)
  • Reliable connectivity: Ensure adequate internet connection for role requirements
  • Availability: Be accessible during agreed working hours

6.2 Communication Expectations

Remote workers must:

  • Responsiveness: Respond to messages within reasonable timeframes (typically same working day)
  • Calendar management: Keep calendar up to date with availability
  • Video meetings: Use video when possible for team meetings and client calls
  • Collaboration: Actively participate in team communications and meetings
  • Visibility: Ensure team and manager are aware of your work and progress

6.3 Work-Life Boundaries

  • Maintain clear boundaries between work and personal time
  • Work contracted hours (not excessive hours)
  • Take regular breaks and lunch breaks
  • Disconnect outside working hours (right to disconnect)
  • Use annual leave entitlement

See the Company's Home Working Policy for comprehensive requirements.

7. Time Tracking and Reporting

7.1 Time Recording Requirements

All employees must accurately record time worked:

What to Record

  • Daily working hours (start and finish times)
  • Time allocated to specific clients or projects
  • Non-billable time (internal work, training, admin)
  • Annual leave and other absences

Recording Standards

  • Accuracy: Record time honestly and accurately
  • Promptness: Record time daily (not retrospectively at end of week/month)
  • Detail: Provide sufficient description of work performed
  • System: Use company-approved time tracking system

7.2 Timesheets and Invoicing

  • Submission: Submit timesheets by required deadlines (typically weekly)
  • Approval: Obtain line manager approval before submission
  • Client billing: Ensure billable time is accurately recorded for client invoicing
  • Honesty: Never inflate or falsify time records

7.3 Working Time Regulations

The Company complies with the Working Time Regulations 1998:

  • Maximum hours: Average 48 hours per week (over 17-week reference period)
  • Rest breaks: 20-minute break if working more than 6 hours
  • Daily rest: 11 consecutive hours rest in each 24-hour period
  • Weekly rest: 24 hours uninterrupted rest per week (or 48 hours per fortnight)

Employees must not regularly exceed these limits. Speak to your line manager if workload requires excessive hours.

8. Quality Standards

8.1 Work Quality

All work produced must meet professional quality standards:

  • Technical excellence: Deliver technically sound solutions and advice
  • Accuracy: Ensure all work is accurate and error-free
  • Completeness: Deliver complete solutions addressing all requirements
  • Timeliness: Meet agreed deadlines and communicate proactively if delays arise
  • Review: Conduct appropriate peer review and quality assurance
  • Documentation: Maintain clear documentation of work performed

8.2 Professional Standards

Where applicable to your role, comply with:

  • Relevant professional body codes of conduct (BCS, IET, etc.)
  • Industry best practices and standards
  • Client-specific quality requirements
  • Regulatory requirements

8.3 Continuous Improvement

Contribute to quality improvement by:

  • Learning from mistakes and near-misses
  • Sharing knowledge and best practices with colleagues
  • Participating in retrospectives and lessons learned
  • Suggesting process improvements
  • Keeping skills and knowledge current

9. Communication Expectations

9.1 Internal Communication

Effective communication within the team is essential:

  • Responsiveness: Respond to emails/messages within 24 hours (same day where possible)
  • Clarity: Communicate clearly and concisely
  • Transparency: Keep manager and team informed of progress, issues, and blockers
  • Meetings: Attend required meetings, arrive on time, contribute actively
  • Documentation: Document decisions, actions, and key information in shared systems
  • Feedback: Provide and receive feedback constructively

9.2 Client Communication

When communicating with clients:

  • Professionalism: Maintain professional tone and manner at all times
  • Responsiveness: Respond to client queries promptly (within 24 hours)
  • Clarity: Explain technical matters in accessible language
  • Escalation: Escalate concerns or issues to line manager appropriately
  • Consistency: Ensure consistency with Company positions and advice
  • Documentation: Keep clear records of client communications and decisions

9.3 Written Communication Standards

  • Use professional language and tone
  • Check spelling, grammar, and formatting
  • Use Company templates where available
  • Include appropriate disclaimers and signatures
  • Be mindful that emails can be disclosed in legal proceedings

10. Professional Development

10.1 Maintaining Competence

You are responsible for:

  • Keeping technical skills and knowledge current
  • Completing mandatory training (compliance, security, etc.)
  • Maintaining relevant professional certifications
  • Identifying personal development needs
  • Actively participating in learning opportunities

10.2 Using Development Budget

  • Make effective use of professional development budget (see Employee Benefits Policy)
  • Plan learning aligned with role requirements and career goals
  • Share learning with colleagues
  • Apply new knowledge and skills in your work

10.3 Knowledge Sharing

Contribute to organizational knowledge by:

  • Documenting solutions, patterns, and lessons learned
  • Presenting at internal knowledge-sharing sessions
  • Mentoring junior colleagues
  • Contributing to internal wikis and documentation
  • Participating in communities of practice

11. Health, Safety and Wellbeing

11.1 Health and Safety Duties

Under the Health and Safety at Work Act 1974, you must:

  • Take reasonable care of your own health and safety
  • Take reasonable care of others who may be affected by your actions
  • Cooperate with the Company on health and safety matters
  • Not misuse anything provided for health and safety
  • Report hazards, near-misses, and incidents

11.2 Display Screen Equipment (DSE)

If you use screens regularly:

  • Complete DSE self-assessment (home and office workstations)
  • Request adjustments or equipment if needed
  • Take regular screen breaks (5-10 minutes per hour)
  • Arrange eye tests every 2 years (Company will pay)
  • Maintain good posture and ergonomic setup

11.3 Mental Health and Wellbeing

  • Prioritize your mental health and wellbeing
  • Use Employee Assistance Programme if needed
  • Speak to manager if work is affecting mental health
  • Take annual leave and disconnect from work
  • Support colleagues' wellbeing

See the Company's Health and Safety Policy for comprehensive requirements.

12. Compliance and Consequences

12.1 Mandatory Compliance

All employees must comply with:

  • This Employee Responsibilities policy
  • All Company policies and procedures
  • Employment contract terms
  • Applicable laws and regulations
  • Client contractual obligations
  • Professional codes of conduct

12.2 Consequences of Non-Compliance

Failure to meet responsibilities may result in:

  • Informal coaching or performance management
  • Formal disciplinary action (verbal warning, written warning, final written warning)
  • Dismissal (including summary dismissal for gross misconduct)
  • Legal action (for breach of confidentiality, data breaches, etc.)
  • Report to professional bodies or authorities (where applicable)

12.3 Gross Misconduct

The following are examples of gross misconduct that may result in summary dismissal:

  • Serious breach of confidentiality
  • Data protection breaches involving intentional misuse of personal data
  • Theft, fraud, or dishonesty
  • Serious breach of health and safety rules
  • Harassment, discrimination, or bullying
  • Serious breach of IT security or acceptable use
  • Being under the influence of alcohol or drugs at work
  • Serious insubordination
  • Falsification of time records or expense claims

13.1 Questions and Guidance

  • Speak to your line manager

13.2 Reporting Breaches

If you become aware of breaches of policies or unethical conduct:

  • Speak to your line manager (if appropriate)
  • Contact HR or senior management
  • Use the Whistleblowing Policy for serious concerns
  • Report data breaches to security@talltree.tech
