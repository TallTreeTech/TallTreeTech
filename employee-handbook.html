3. Anti-Harassment and Bullying

3.1 Policy Statement

TallTree Technologies is committed to providing a working environment free from harassment and bullying. We will not tolerate harassment or bullying of any kind, whether in the physical workplace, during remote work, at work-related events, or in work-related communications.

3.2 What is Harassment?

Harassment is unwanted conduct related to a protected characteristic (age, disability, gender reassignment, race, religion or belief, sex, sexual orientation, marriage and civil partnership, pregnancy and maternity) that:

  • Violates a person's dignity; or
  • Creates an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating, or offensive environment

3.3 What is Bullying?

Bullying is offensive, intimidating, malicious, or insulting behaviour that makes a person feel vulnerable, upset, humiliated, undermined, or threatened. Examples include:

  • Shouting at, being sarcastic towards, or ridiculing an individual
  • Persistent unjustified criticism
  • Deliberately undermining or ignoring someone
  • Spreading malicious rumours
  • Exclusion from work-related events or communications
  • Setting impossible deadlines or unachievable tasks

3.4 Examples of Harassment

Harassment may include (but is not limited to):

  • Unwanted physical contact
  • Offensive jokes, banter, or comments
  • Display or circulation of offensive materials or images
  • Unwelcome sexual advances or requests for sexual favours
  • Intrusive questions about private life
  • Mockery, threats, or insults
  • Exclusion or victimization
  • Online harassment through email, messaging, or social media

3.5 What to Do if You Experience Harassment or Bullying

If you experience or witness harassment or bullying:

  • Informal approach: If you feel able, you may tell the person that their behaviour is unwelcome and ask them to stop
  • Speak to your manager: Discuss the situation with your line manager (unless they are involved)
  • Speak to HR: Contact HR for confidential advice and support
  • Formal complaint: Raise a formal grievance (see Section 12)
  • Serious cases: In serious cases, use the whistleblowing procedure (see Section 14)

3.6 Investigation and Action

All complaints will be taken seriously and investigated promptly, fairly, and confidentially. Where harassment or bullying is found to have occurred, appropriate action will be taken, which may include disciplinary action up to and including dismissal.

3.7 Protection from Retaliation

Employees who make complaints in good faith will not suffer any detriment or victimization as a result. Victimization of complainants or witnesses will be treated as a serious disciplinary matter.

4. Remote Working

4.1 Remote-First Culture

TallTree Technologies operates as a fully remote organization. All employees work remotely as standard, with occasional in-person meetings or events as required.

4.2 Home Workspace Requirements

When working from home, you must ensure your workspace:

  • Is suitable for focused work with minimal distractions
  • Has adequate lighting, heating, and ventilation
  • Has suitable furniture, including a proper desk and chair (see Section 15 for ergonomic guidance)
  • Is safe and free from hazards
  • Has reliable high-speed internet connectivity
  • Allows for confidential conversations and video calls
  • Maintains security and confidentiality of Company information

4.3 Workspace Self-Assessment

All remote workers must complete a home workspace self-assessment (Display Screen Equipment - DSE assessment) to ensure their setup meets health and safety requirements. Contact HR for the assessment form and guidance.

4.4 Equipment and Technology

The Company will provide:

  • Laptop or computer as required for your role
  • Necessary software licenses and tools
  • Access to cloud-based collaboration platforms
  • Additional equipment as reasonably required (subject to approval)

You are responsible for:

  • Maintaining equipment in good condition
  • Reporting any faults or issues promptly
  • Ensuring equipment security and not allowing unauthorized use
  • Providing your own reliable internet connection
  • Returning all equipment upon termination of employment

4.5 Working Location

Your primary working location should be your home address in the UK (as notified to the Company). If you wish to work from another location:

  • Temporary changes (e.g., working while visiting family): Notify your manager
  • Permanent relocation: Must be approved by HR and may require contract amendments
  • Working from abroad: Requires prior approval due to tax, immigration, and insurance implications

4.6 Insurance and Tax Implications

You should:

  • Inform your home insurer that you are working from home
  • Ensure you have adequate home and contents insurance
  • Be aware that you may be able to claim tax relief for working from home expenses
  • If you own your home, check whether working from home affects your mortgage terms
  • If you rent, check your tenancy agreement permits home working

4.7 Availability and Response Times

During your working hours, you should:

  • Be available via email, messaging platforms (e.g., Slack, Teams), and video conferencing
  • Respond to messages within a reasonable timeframe (typically within 2-4 hours during working hours)
  • Update your status on communication platforms to indicate availability
  • Notify your team if you will be unavailable during working hours

5. Communication and Collaboration

5.1 Communication Tools

We use various tools for communication and collaboration. You are expected to:

  • Check email regularly (at least twice daily during working hours)
  • Use designated messaging platforms for real-time communication
  • Attend video meetings with camera on (where possible and appropriate)
  • Use project management and collaboration tools as required
  • Keep calendars up to date and accept/decline meeting invitations promptly

5.2 Video Meetings

Best practices for video meetings:

  • Be on time and prepared
  • Test your audio and video before important meetings
  • Use a professional background or background blur
  • Mute when not speaking in large meetings
  • Be mindful of what's visible in your background
  • Dress appropriately (smart casual as a minimum for client meetings)
  • Give others your full attention (avoid multitasking)

5.3 Email Etiquette

  • Use clear, concise subject lines
  • Keep emails professional and courteous
  • Respond within 24 hours during working days (or set an out-of-office)
  • Use appropriate salutations and sign-offs
  • Proofread before sending
  • Don't use ALL CAPS (it's perceived as shouting)
  • Be careful with "Reply All"
  • Think before you send – email is permanent

5.4 Messaging Etiquette

  • Use instant messaging for quick questions and informal communication
  • Respect others' status (e.g., "Do Not Disturb", "In a Meeting")
  • Avoid messaging outside working hours unless urgent
  • Use threads to keep conversations organized
  • Use appropriate channels (not DMs for company-wide information)
  • Keep it professional – banter is fine, but be respectful

5.5 Documentation and Knowledge Sharing

Good documentation is essential in a remote environment:

  • Document important decisions and discussions
  • Keep project documentation up to date
  • Share knowledge through wikis, documentation repositories, or other approved platforms
  • Write clear commit messages and code comments
  • Create handover documentation when taking leave or changing roles

6. Working Hours and Flexibility

6.1 Standard Working Hours

The Company's standard working hours are set out in your employment contract (typically 37.5 hours per week, Monday to Friday). While we offer flexibility, you should generally be available during core business hours (typically 10:00 AM - 4:00 PM UK time) unless otherwise agreed.

6.2 Flexible Working

We support flexible working arrangements to help you balance work and personal commitments. You may request:

  • Flexible start and finish times
  • Compressed hours (e.g., working full-time hours over fewer days)
  • Part-time working
  • Other arrangements that suit your circumstances

All employees have the statutory right to request flexible working after 26 weeks of employment. Requests will be considered carefully and will be approved where business needs permit.

6.3 Time Recording

You may be required to record your working time in accordance with Working Time Regulations. This helps ensure compliance with rest break and maximum working hours requirements.

6.4 Breaks and Rest Periods

Under the Working Time Regulations 1998, you are entitled to:

  • A minimum 20-minute unpaid break if you work more than 6 hours in a day
  • A minimum 11 consecutive hours rest in each 24-hour period
  • An uninterrupted rest period of 24 hours in each 7-day period (or 48 hours in 14 days)

We encourage you to take regular breaks throughout the day to maintain wellbeing and productivity.

6.5 Overtime and Additional Hours

From time to time, you may need to work beyond your contracted hours to meet business needs. Your approach to overtime is set out in your employment contract and may vary depending on your role and level.

6.6 Right to Disconnect

While we are a remote-first company, we respect your right to disconnect outside working hours. You are not expected to:

  • Respond to emails or messages outside your working hours
  • Work during holidays or rest days (unless in exceptional circumstances and with prior agreement)
  • Be "always on" just because you work from home

If you choose to send emails outside normal hours, consider using scheduling features to deliver them during working hours. If you need to contact someone urgently outside hours, telephone them directly.

7. Data Protection and Information Security

7.1 Data Protection Principles

The Company processes personal data in accordance with the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018. As an employee, you may process personal data relating to:

  • Clients and customers
  • Employees and job applicants
  • Suppliers and partners
  • Others

7.2 Employee Responsibilities

When processing personal data, you must:

  • Only access personal data that you need for your work
  • Only use personal data for legitimate business purposes
  • Keep personal data secure and confidential
  • Not share personal data with unauthorized persons
  • Not remove personal data from Company systems without authorization
  • Follow the Company's data protection policies and procedures
  • Complete mandatory data protection training
  • Report any data breaches immediately (see Section 7.5)

7.3 Information Security

To protect Company and client information, you must:

  • Use strong, unique passwords for all systems and change them regularly
  • Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA) where available
  • Lock your computer when away from your desk (even at home)
  • Never share your login credentials with anyone
  • Only use Company-approved software and services
  • Encrypt sensitive data in transit and at rest
  • Be cautious of phishing emails and suspicious links
  • Keep software and operating systems up to date
  • Use approved cloud storage (not personal cloud accounts for work data)
  • Never store work data on personal devices without encryption and approval

7.4 Remote Working Security

When working remotely:

  • Ensure your home Wi-Fi is password-protected and uses strong encryption (WPA2 or WPA3)
  • Don't use public Wi-Fi for work without a VPN
  • Be aware of who can see your screen when working in public spaces
  • Don't leave devices unattended
  • Be careful about discussing confidential matters where others can overhear
  • Ensure family members cannot access your work devices
  • Use privacy screens on laptops if working in public

7.5 Data Breach Reporting

A data breach is any incident where personal data is lost, stolen, accessed by unauthorized persons, or otherwise compromised. If you become aware of an actual or suspected data breach, you must:

  • Report it immediately to your line manager and HR
  • Do not try to fix it yourself or investigate further
  • Preserve evidence (e.g., don't delete emails)
  • Follow instructions from the incident response team

We must report certain data breaches to the ICO within 72 hours, so speed is critical.

7.6 Confidential Information

Your obligations regarding confidential information are set out in your employment contract and NDA. Remember that confidentiality obligations continue after your employment ends.

12. Grievance Procedure

12.1 Purpose

This procedure provides a fair and transparent way for you to raise concerns about your work, working environment, or relationships with colleagues. This procedure complies with the ACAS Code of Practice on Disciplinary and Grievance Procedures.

12.2 What is a Grievance?

A grievance is a concern, problem, or complaint that you raise with your employer. Examples include:

  • Terms and conditions of employment
  • Health and safety
  • Work relations and working environment
  • Bullying or harassment
  • Discrimination
  • Organizational change

12.3 Informal Resolution

Many issues can be resolved informally. You are encouraged to:

  • Discuss the issue with your line manager (if appropriate)
  • Seek informal mediation or discussion to resolve the matter
  • Contact HR for confidential advice

If informal resolution is not successful or appropriate, you may raise a formal grievance.

12.4 Formal Grievance - Step 1: Raising a Grievance

To raise a formal grievance:

  • Put your grievance in writing to your line manager (or to HR if your grievance is about your line manager)
  • Include specific details: what happened, when, where, who was involved, and how it has affected you
  • State what outcome you are seeking
  • Submit your grievance as soon as reasonably possible after the issue arises

12.5 Formal Grievance - Step 2: Investigation

Once a grievance is received:

  • The Company will acknowledge receipt within [e.g., 5] working days
  • An appropriate manager (not involved in the grievance) will be appointed to investigate
  • The investigator may interview you, witnesses, and others as necessary
  • You may be asked to provide additional information or evidence
  • The investigation will be conducted promptly, fairly, and confidentially

12.6 Formal Grievance - Step 3: Grievance Meeting

You will be invited to a grievance meeting to discuss your grievance:

  • You will receive at least [e.g., 5] working days' notice of the meeting
  • The meeting will be conducted by the investigating manager
  • You have the right to be accompanied by a work colleague or trade union representative
  • You will have the opportunity to explain your grievance and present evidence
  • Notes will be taken and provided to you for review

12.7 Formal Grievance - Step 4: Decision

After the meeting:

  • The manager will consider all evidence and reach a decision
  • You will be notified of the decision in writing within [e.g., 10] working days of the meeting
  • The decision letter will explain the decision, any actions to be taken, and your right of appeal

12.8 Right to Appeal

If you are not satisfied with the outcome, you may appeal:

  • Submit your appeal in writing within [e.g., 5] working days of receiving the decision
  • State clearly the grounds for your appeal
  • An appeal meeting will be arranged with a senior manager not previously involved
  • You have the right to be accompanied at the appeal meeting
  • The appeal decision is final

12.9 Right to be Accompanied

You have the right to be accompanied at grievance and appeal meetings by:

  • A work colleague; or
  • A trade union representative

You must make your own arrangements for a companion and inform the Company of your companion's details in advance.

12.10 Confidentiality

Grievances will be handled confidentially. Information will only be shared with those who need to know for the purposes of investigation and resolution.

13. Disciplinary Procedure

13.1 Purpose

This procedure sets out how the Company will handle disciplinary matters fairly and consistently, in line with the ACAS Code of Practice on Disciplinary and Grievance Procedures. The aim is to encourage improvement in conduct or performance rather than to punish.

13.2 Scope

This procedure applies to all employees. Different procedures may apply during probationary periods, where shorter timescales and processes may be used.

13.3 Minor Issues

Minor issues of conduct or performance are usually dealt with informally through:

  • Informal discussion with your manager
  • Coaching and guidance
  • Additional training or support

Informal action is not part of the formal disciplinary procedure and will not result in disciplinary sanctions.

13.4 Examples of Misconduct

Misconduct that may lead to formal disciplinary action includes:

  • Unsatisfactory work performance despite support and warnings
  • Poor timekeeping or attendance
  • Failure to follow reasonable instructions
  • Breach of Company policies or procedures
  • Inappropriate behavior or language
  • Minor breaches of confidentiality
  • Failure to meet deadlines or standards

13.5 Examples of Gross Misconduct

Gross misconduct is misconduct so serious that it may justify summary dismissal (dismissal without notice). Examples include:

  • Theft, fraud, or dishonesty
  • Physical violence or threats of violence
  • Serious bullying or harassment
  • Deliberate damage to Company property
  • Serious breach of confidentiality or data protection
  • Serious misuse of Company systems or resources
  • Being under the influence of alcohol or illegal drugs at work
  • Serious insubordination
  • Bringing the Company into serious disrepute
  • Serious breach of health and safety rules
  • Unauthorized disclosure of confidential information
  • Serious or repeated discrimination or harassment

This list is not exhaustive. The Company will determine whether conduct amounts to gross misconduct based on the circumstances of each case.

13.6 Suspension

In serious cases, you may be suspended on full pay while an investigation is carried out. Suspension is a neutral act and does not imply guilt. During suspension, you must:

  • Remain available to attend meetings as required
  • Not contact colleagues, clients, or others in relation to the matter under investigation (unless authorized)
  • Not access Company systems or premises (unless authorized)

13.7 Investigation

Before any disciplinary action is taken, the Company will:

  • Conduct a fair and thorough investigation
  • Gather relevant evidence and interview witnesses
  • Give you the opportunity to provide your account
  • Determine whether there is a case to answer

13.8 Disciplinary Meeting

If the investigation suggests there is a case to answer, you will be invited to a disciplinary meeting:

  • You will receive written notice at least [e.g., 5] working days in advance
  • The notice will include details of the alleged misconduct, possible consequences, and supporting evidence
  • You have the right to be accompanied by a work colleague or trade union representative
  • The meeting will be conducted by an appropriate manager
  • You will have the opportunity to state your case, present evidence, and call witnesses
  • The meeting may be adjourned to allow further investigation if necessary

13.9 Disciplinary Sanctions

Following the disciplinary meeting, possible outcomes include:

  • No action: If the allegation is not upheld
  • First written warning: For a first instance of misconduct (usually active for 6-12 months)
  • Final written warning: For serious or repeated misconduct (usually active for 12 months)
  • Dismissal with notice: For serious or repeated misconduct
  • Summary dismissal: For gross misconduct (dismissal without notice or pay in lieu)

Other actions may include demotion, transfer, or other sanctions appropriate to the circumstances.

13.10 Notification of Decision

You will be notified of the decision in writing within [e.g., 5-10] working days. The letter will include:

  • Details of the misconduct
  • The sanction imposed and duration (if applicable)
  • The improvement expected
  • The consequences of further misconduct
  • The right of appeal

13.11 Right to Appeal

You have the right to appeal against any disciplinary sanction:

  • Submit your appeal in writing within [e.g., 5] working days of receiving the decision
  • State clearly the grounds for your appeal
  • An appeal meeting will be arranged with a senior manager not previously involved
  • You have the right to be accompanied at the appeal meeting
  • The appeal decision is final

13.12 Right to be Accompanied

You have the statutory right to be accompanied at disciplinary and appeal meetings by:

  • A work colleague; or
  • A trade union representative

14. Whistleblowing Policy

14.1 Purpose

This policy provides a mechanism for employees to raise concerns about wrongdoing within the Company without fear of retaliation. It complies with the Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998 (PIDA).

14.2 What is Whistleblowing?

Whistleblowing is the disclosure of information about wrongdoing that is in the public interest. Examples of matters that may be raised under this policy include:

  • Criminal offenses (e.g., fraud, theft, corruption)
  • Failure to comply with legal obligations
  • Miscarriages of justice
  • Danger to health and safety
  • Damage to the environment
  • Deliberate concealment of information about any of the above

14.3 When to Use This Policy

Use this policy if you have concerns about serious wrongdoing that is in the public interest. Do not use this policy for:

  • Personal employment grievances (use the grievance procedure instead)
  • Minor breaches of policy that don't affect the public interest

14.4 How to Raise a Concern

You may raise concerns in the following ways:

  • To your line manager: Unless they are involved in the wrongdoing
  • To a senior manager or director: If your line manager is involved or you don't feel comfortable approaching them
  • To HR: For confidential discussion and advice
  • Via the whistleblowing hotline/email: [Provide contact details if available]

When raising a concern, provide as much detail as possible:

  • Background and history of the concern
  • Names, dates, places, and details of events
  • The reason why you are particularly concerned
  • Any evidence you have to support your concern

14.5 Investigation

Once a concern is raised:

  • The Company will acknowledge receipt and assess the concern
  • An appropriate person will be appointed to investigate
  • The investigation will be thorough, fair, and confidential
  • You may be asked for additional information
  • You will be informed of the outcome where appropriate (subject to confidentiality and legal constraints)

14.6 Confidentiality

The Company will treat all concerns confidentially and will not disclose your identity without your consent unless:

  • Required by law
  • Necessary for the investigation or any subsequent proceedings

You may raise concerns anonymously, although this may limit the Company's ability to investigate fully.

14.7 Protection from Detriment

You will not suffer any detriment for raising a genuine concern in good faith. This means you will not be:

  • Dismissed
  • Disciplined
  • Subject to any other detriment (e.g., denial of promotion, training, or benefits)

Any employee who retaliates against a whistleblower will face disciplinary action, which may include dismissal.

14.8 Malicious or False Allegations

If you make an allegation in bad faith (e.g., for personal gain or to cause harm) or knowingly make a false allegation, you may face disciplinary action.

14.9 External Disclosure

The Company encourages you to raise concerns internally first. However, you may make a protected disclosure to a prescribed regulator (e.g., ICO, HSE, FCA) or, in exceptional cases, to other external bodies, in accordance with PIDA. Seek legal advice before making external disclosures.

15. Health, Safety and Wellbeing

15.1 Health and Safety Policy

The Company is committed to providing a safe and healthy working environment and complying with the Health and Safety at Work Act 1974 and related legislation. This applies to remote working as well as any physical premises.

15.2 Employee Responsibilities

All employees have a legal duty to:

  • Take reasonable care of your own health and safety and that of others
  • Comply with health and safety policies and procedures
  • Report any hazards, accidents, or near-misses
  • Use equipment safely and as instructed
  • Not interfere with or misuse anything provided for health and safety

15.3 Remote Working - Health and Safety

When working remotely, you must:

  • Complete a home workspace self-assessment (DSE assessment)
  • Ensure your workspace is safe and suitable
  • Use appropriate furniture and equipment
  • Take regular breaks and avoid prolonged screen time
  • Maintain good posture and ergonomics
  • Ensure adequate lighting and ventilation
  • Keep walkways and exits clear
  • Report any health and safety concerns to your manager

15.4 Display Screen Equipment (DSE)

If you use display screen equipment (computers, laptops) for prolonged periods:

  • You are entitled to a DSE assessment
  • Adjust your screen, keyboard, and chair for comfort
  • Position your screen to avoid glare and reflections
  • Take regular breaks (5-10 minutes every hour)
  • You may request an eye test (costs reimbursed by the Company)
  • If prescribed, the Company will contribute towards glasses for DSE use

15.5 Accident and Incident Reporting

You must report all work-related accidents, injuries, near-misses, and hazards to your line manager and HR as soon as possible, even if working remotely. This helps us prevent future incidents and comply with legal reporting requirements (RIDDOR).

15.6 First Aid

When working remotely, you are responsible for ensuring you have access to first aid facilities in case of injury. The Company cannot provide first aid cover for remote workers, but we can provide guidance on setting up a basic first aid kit at home.

15.7 Mental Health and Wellbeing

The Company is committed to supporting employee mental health and wellbeing. We provide:

  • Open culture where mental health concerns can be discussed
  • Flexible working arrangements to support wellbeing
  • Access to Employee Assistance Programme (EAP) [if provided]
  • Mental health first aiders [if available]
  • Resources and training on mental health awareness

If you are experiencing mental health difficulties:

  • Speak to your manager or HR in confidence
  • Contact the EAP for confidential counseling and support
  • Request reasonable adjustments if needed
  • Consider speaking to your GP

15.8 Stress and Burnout

Remote working can blur boundaries between work and personal life. To prevent stress and burnout:

  • Set clear boundaries between work and personal time
  • Take regular breaks and use your holiday entitlement
  • Don't work excessive hours consistently
  • Speak up if your workload is unmanageable
  • Stay connected with colleagues to avoid isolation
  • Maintain healthy habits (exercise, sleep, nutrition)

15.9 Lone Working

Remote workers are typically lone workers. To stay safe:

  • Ensure someone knows your working schedule
  • Have a means of raising an alarm in an emergency
  • If visiting clients or working from unfamiliar locations, notify your manager and check in afterward
  • If you feel unsafe at any time, remove yourself from the situation and contact emergency services if necessary

16. IT Acceptable Use

16.1 Purpose

This policy sets out acceptable use of the Company's IT systems, equipment, and resources, including computers, laptops, email, internet, software, and cloud services.

16.2 Acceptable Use

Company IT systems are provided for business purposes. You may:

  • Use email and internet for work-related purposes
  • Make reasonable incidental personal use (e.g., checking personal email during breaks)
  • Use approved software and cloud services
  • Store work-related data on approved systems

16.3 Unacceptable Use

You must not:

  • Use Company systems for illegal activities
  • Access, download, store, or distribute offensive, defamatory, discriminatory, or obscene material
  • Install unauthorized software or hardware
  • Deliberately introduce viruses, malware, or other harmful code
  • Attempt to bypass security measures or access unauthorized systems
  • Use Company systems to run a personal business
  • Use excessive bandwidth for personal activities (e.g., streaming, gaming)
  • Share login credentials or allow unauthorized use of your account
  • Use Company systems to harass, bully, or discriminate against others

16.4 Email Use

  • Use your Company email address for work-related communications
  • Do not use personal email for work purposes (to avoid data security issues)
  • Be professional and courteous in all email communications
  • Remember that emails can be disclosed in legal proceedings
  • Do not open suspicious emails or attachments (report them to IT)
  • Do not send large attachments without considering alternatives (e.g., file sharing links)

16.5 Internet Use

  • Internet access is primarily for business use
  • Reasonable personal use is permitted during breaks
  • Do not visit inappropriate, illegal, or time-wasting websites
  • Be aware that internet usage may be monitored

16.6 Software and Cloud Services

  • Only use approved software and cloud services
  • Do not install unauthorized software (including browser extensions)
  • Do not use personal cloud storage (e.g., personal Dropbox, Google Drive) for work data
  • Comply with software licensing terms
  • Request approval before using new tools or services

16.7 Monitoring

The Company reserves the right to monitor use of IT systems for legitimate business purposes, including:

  • Ensuring compliance with policies
  • Preventing and detecting crime
  • Protecting systems and data
  • Investigating misconduct

Monitoring will be carried out in accordance with data protection laws. You should have no expectation of privacy when using Company systems.

16.8 Bring Your Own Device (BYOD)

If you use personal devices for work (subject to Company policy and approval):

  • You must comply with security requirements (e.g., encryption, passcode)
  • The Company may require the ability to remotely wipe Company data
  • You are responsible for the security and insurance of your device
  • The Company is not responsible for damage to or loss of personal devices

16.9 Breach of Policy

Breach of this policy may result in disciplinary action up to and including dismissal. Serious breaches (e.g., illegal activity, serious security breaches) may also result in criminal or civil action.

17. Leave and Absence

17.1 Annual Leave

Your annual leave entitlement is set out in your employment contract. To book annual leave:

  • Request leave through the Company's booking system
  • Give adequate notice (typically at least 2 weeks for short periods, longer for extended leave)
  • Obtain manager approval before booking travel
  • Plan leave to minimize impact on the business
  • Ensure adequate coverage for your responsibilities

17.2 Sickness Absence

Sickness absence procedures are set out in your employment contract. Key points:

  • Notify your manager as soon as possible on the first day of absence
  • Provide regular updates on your condition and expected return date
  • Provide a fit note for absences exceeding 7 days
  • Attend occupational health appointments if requested
  • On return, complete a return-to-work meeting with your manager

17.3 Family Leave

You are entitled to various types of family-related leave. Contact HR for details and to request:

  • Maternity leave and pay
  • Paternity leave and pay
  • Adoption leave and pay
  • Shared parental leave and pay
  • Parental leave (unpaid leave for parents of children under 18)
  • Parental bereavement leave and pay
  • Time off for dependants (emergency unpaid leave)

17.4 Other Leave

  • Jury service: You must notify the Company immediately if called for jury service. You will be given time off, and the Company may reclaim any jury service allowance paid to you.
  • Medical appointments: Where possible, schedule appointments outside working hours. If not possible, notify your manager and minimize time away from work.
  • Bereavement leave: In the event of bereavement, speak to your manager about compassionate leave. The amount granted will depend on the circumstances and your relationship to the deceased.
  • Public duties: Time off may be granted for certain public duties (e.g., magistrate duties, local council duties). Discuss with your manager.

17.5 Unauthorized Absence

Absence without permission or proper notification is unauthorized and may result in:

  • Non-payment of salary for the period of absence
  • Disciplinary action, potentially leading to dismissal
